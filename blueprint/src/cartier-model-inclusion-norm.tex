\section{The original Cartier inclusion norm is the intrinsic ideal norm}

\begin{theorem}[Actual Cartier inclusion metric identity]
\label{thm:actual-cartier-inclusion-model-norm}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelHomNorm_idealInclusion}
\leanok
\uses{cor:actual-local-spectrum-framed-ideal-principal,lem:actual-model-ideal-center-pullback-norm,def:scheme-line-actual-model-hom-norm,lem:cartier-actual-invertible-module-sheaf}
Let $I$ be an actual invertible ideal sheaf on an actual proper
model. The norm of its actual whole line-module inclusion into the
original structure sheaf, for the genuine original proper-model
line norms, equals the intrinsic original model ideal norm at every
original relative scheme-valued point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual pullback line to the original residue-field valuation
ring has an actual global unit frame, by invertibility and locality
of that actual spectrum. For the original structure line use the
native original pullback-unit frame. The whole module-morphism
norm is the original residue valuation of its actual coefficient
in these two genuine frames. By the entire local-spectrum ideal
pullback image theorem, the actual geometric pullback ideal is
exactly the principal ideal generated by this same coefficient:
the actual inclusion, applied to the inverse-frame unit section.
The intrinsic original ideal norm equals the ideal norm of that
entire geometric ideal at the actual center-section spectrum. The
bounded norm of its principal ideal is exactly the original value
of its generator. This is the same coefficient valuation which
computes the actual line-morphism norm. All frames were derived
from original invertibility; no compatibility certificate, flatness,
nonvanishing hypothesis, or extra model datum is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Actual logarithmic metric identity]
\label{cor:actual-cartier-inclusion-model-log}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelHomNorm_idealInclusion_log}
\leanok
\uses{thm:actual-cartier-inclusion-model-norm}
The extended-real logarithm of the original Cartier inclusion norm
is the intrinsic original model ideal logarithm.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the genuine extended-real logarithm to the preceding norm
identity. This also includes zero norms.
\end{proof}
