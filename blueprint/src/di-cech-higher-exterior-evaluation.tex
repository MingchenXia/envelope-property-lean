\section{The original affine leading comparison for the higher Witt maps}

Use the original $Y,g,P,K,T$, canonical Witt cover, and actual maps $\Phi_q$
constructed in the preceding higher-exterior chapter. All evaluations and units
below retain those same original objects and scalar actions.

\begin{lemma}[The original unit preserves the last-entry exterior product]
\label{lem:di-higher-exterior-unit-product}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.DI.CechHigherExteriorUnitProducts.wedgeProduct_init_last, EnvelopeProperty.DI.CechHigherExteriorUnitProducts.targetUnit, EnvelopeProperty.DI.CechHigherExteriorUnitProducts.targetUnit_product, EnvelopeProperty.DI.CechHigherExteriorUnitProducts.targetUnit_init_last}
\leanok
For an original module presheaf $P$, a tuple $(m_0,\ldots,m_q)$ on an open
$V$, and the original exterior sheafification units $\eta_j$, the product
$\eta_q(m_0\wedge\cdots\wedge m_{q-1})\wedge\eta_1(m_q)$ equals
$\eta_{q+1}(m_0\wedge\cdots\wedge m_q)$, after the equality
$1+q=q+1$ of its degree indices. The same identity holds after restriction
along an equality of original opens.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The original sheaf product on two unit images is the unit image of the
original homogeneous exterior product. Its inclusion into the exterior
algebra is literal multiplication. The product of the first $q$ generators
and the last generator is the product of the whole ordered tuple, by the
last-entry formula for a finite product. Injectivity of the homogeneous
submodule inclusion gives equality of the raw exterior elements. The
sheafification unit gives the asserted section equality. Restriction of
the original product is the product of the original restrictions; applying
this equality to the original open-equality restriction gives the final
assertion, with every degree transport unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The chosen Witt lift preserves the ordered source-unit product]
\label{lem:di-higher-exterior-witt-unit-product}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.model_rawUnit, EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.model_rawUnit_value, EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.model_rawUnit_product}
\uses{lem:di-higher-exterior-unit-product,cor:di-actual-witt-exterior-leading-cartier}
Let $U$ be an affine member of the smooth Witt lifting, choose its original
etale coordinates and a lift $F$ of residue Frobenius, and let $W$ be the
corresponding original twisted special-fibre open. Write $C^q$ for the
original affine exterior map of that same $F$, and $\eta_q$ for the
original exterior sheafification unit on $W$. For every $q\geq0$ and every
raw tuple $(m_0,\ldots,m_q)$ on $W$,
\[
 C^{q+1}\eta_{q+1}(m_0,\ldots,m_q)
 =C^q\eta_q(m_0,\ldots,m_{q-1})\wedge C^1\eta_1(m_q),
\]
where the right side uses the original pushed-form product and the equality
$1+q=q+1$ of degree indices. This assertion requires no bound $q<p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The original cotangent comparison is induced by the original coefficient
square and the original affine section-ring isomorphism. Its exterior
extension sends the original raw wedge of the $m_j$ to the native wedge
of their images under that same cotangent comparison. Composing the inverse
of the original exterior-unit isomorphism with the forward unit therefore
identifies $\eta_q(m)$ with the inverse native-domain image of this native
wedge. The proved source-wedge formula for $C^q$ sends it to the original
target unit of the ordered wedge of the divided one-forms of $F$ applied
to these same cotangent images. Applying the preceding lemma to the initial
$q$ entries and the last entry gives the stated formula, including the
original relative-preimage restriction. All steps retain the original
coefficient inclusion and its induced module actions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The original affine leading representative]
\label{thm:di-higher-exterior-witt-leading-component}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.memberExterior, EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.memberOne, EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.memberOrdered, EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.memberHigher_raw, EnvelopeProperty.DI.ActualWittHigherExteriorEvaluation.memberHigher}
\leanok
\uses{lem:di-higher-exterior-witt-unit-product,lem:di-higher-exterior-unit-product,thm:di-higher-exterior-witt-maps,cor:di-actual-witt-exterior-leading-cartier,def:native-cech-ordered-cup,thm:di-total-cup-original-factor-evaluation,lem:di-reused-native-normalization,lem:di-cech-total-zero-row-projection}
Fix a member $W_i$ of the original canonical Witt cover and its internally
chosen lift $F_i$. Let $e_i:T|_{W_i}\to K|_{W_i}$ be the original local
evaluation, obtained from the zero-Cech-row projection and the actual
restriction--pushforward counit. For $0<q<p$, the degree-$q$ component
of $\Phi_q|_{W_i}e_i$ on sections is the entire original affine exterior
map $C_i^q$ obtained from the published native exterior extension of the
same divided Frobenius $F_i$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\leanok
The original degree-one component has zero-row part the adjoint of the
canonical $\xi_i$. The definition of that canonical component transports
the original member map only along the singleton-intersection equality.
The actual counit triangle therefore gives
$e_i^1\tau^1=\xi_i$ as a whole over-open sheaf morphism. The proved
actual-Witt one-form comparison identifies this same $\xi_i$ on sections
with $C_i^1$; no new local lift or coefficient identification is chosen.

The original total product evaluated in Cech degree zero can only use
zero-Cech-degree factors. Its Koszul coefficient is then one. The actual
total-product projection formula and the original target-unit product
formula thus show, by induction through the fixed ordered recursion,
that $e_i^q\mu_q$ is the ordered exterior product of these same one-form
images. The published source-wedge formula for $C_i^q$ is exactly that
product on each original source wedge. This last equality can also be
read in the ambient exterior algebra: multiplication is associative,
and the native ordered-product formula for $\iota_{\mathrm{multi}}$
identifies the product on an initial tuple followed by its last entry
with the product on the whole tuple. The degree-equality transports
retain the same ambient exterior-algebra value.

Let $a_i(v)=C_i^q(\eta_q(v))$ on the original raw cotangent inputs.
It is alternating because $\eta_q$ is alternating and $C_i^q$ is linear.
The preceding induction proves the whole multilinear equality
$e_i^q\mu_q=a_i$, rather than assuming it. The existing recovery theorem,
with the actual structural map $k\to\mathcal O_Y(W_i)$ and $q<p$, gives
$e_i^q\phi_q(\eta_q(v))=C_i^q(\eta_q(v))$ on every raw wedge.
The factorial inverse is taken in the original field and then mapped
to the original section ring, so this argument also covers empty opens.

For completeness, these values determine the entire section map on
this affine open. The actual affine exterior-unit comparison identifies
$\Gamma(W_i,\Omega_Y^q)$ with the original exterior power
$\bigwedge^q_{\mathcal O_Y(W_i)}P(W_i)$, retaining its original unit map.
Simple wedges span that module over the original ring
$\mathcal O_Y(W_i)$. The native chart and source-unit formulas above
identify the value on each such original wedge, and linearity therefore
gives the entire equality on $\Gamma(W_i,\Omega_Y^q)$. This generation argument
is used only on the genuine affine member, not on arbitrary global opens.

\end{proof}
