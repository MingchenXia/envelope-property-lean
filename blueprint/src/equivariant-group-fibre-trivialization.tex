\section{The entire equivariant unit-fibre trivialization}
\label{sec:equivariant-group-fibre-trivialization}

\begin{theorem}[An equivariant map to the regular group is a product {\cite[\S1.2]{BJ22}}]
\label{thm:equivariant-group-fibre-trivialization}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.normalize, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.toFibre, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.toFibre_inclusion, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.fromProduct, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.toProduct, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.iso, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.iso_hom_fst, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.iso_inv_projection, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreTrivialization.iso_hom_snd_inclusion}
\leanok
In any Cartesian category, let a genuine group object $G$ act
on $Z$, and let the original $p:Z\to G$ be equivariant for
the regular multiplication action of $G$ on itself.
Let $F\to Z$ be the \emph{specified original Cartesian fibre}
of $p$ over the group unit. There is an internally constructed
isomorphism of the entire original objects
\[
 Z\simeq G\times F,
 \qquad z\longmapsto (p(z),p(z)^{-1}z),
 \qquad (g,w)\longmapsto gw.
\]
Its first whole projection is exactly the original $p$;
its second projection followed by the original fibre inclusion
is exactly the inverse-parameter action on $Z$.
No supplied trivialization, integrality or normality is assumed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All formulas below are morphisms in the original Cartesian
category. They may be checked after composition with arbitrary
original test objects, since their products and Cartesian
fibres have their genuine universal properties.
Define $n(z)=p(z)^{-1}z$ using the original action and group
inverse. Equivariance gives
$p(n(z))=p(z)^{-1}p(z)=1$ on the entire original object.
The original Cartesian fibre universal property therefore
gives a unique morphism $n_F:Z\to F$ whose composite with
the original fibre inclusion is $n$. Define
$a:Z\to G\times F$ by the two original components $p,n_F$,
and define $b:G\times F\to Z$ by the original action.

The original group action law gives
$b(a(z))=p(z)(p(z)^{-1}z)=z$ on the entire original $Z$.
For the other composite, equivariance gives $p(gw)=g$
since $p(w)=1$ on the specified fibre. Also
$p(gw)^{-1}(gw)=g^{-1}(gw)=w$. The first component and the
original fibre inclusion therefore identify $a(b(g,w))$
with $(g,w)$; the second fibre-base component is the unique
morphism to the terminal object. Cartesian uniqueness gives
the actual whole inverse equation. Thus $a,b$ are genuinely
mutually inverse morphisms of the original objects, retaining
both original projection equations as claimed.
\end{proof}
