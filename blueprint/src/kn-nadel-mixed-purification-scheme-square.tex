\section{The original purification square over the original base}

Let $A$ be a $k$-algebra, let $B$ be the original mixed root algebra,
let $S$ be the entire original unit-root algebra, and put
$C=S\otimes_A B$.  Let $P$ be the original pure target and let
$\rho:C\to P$ be the ring map of the original produced purification.
Every factor of the original root family is retained.

\begin{lemma}[The original purification retains every unit-base coefficient]
\label{lem:kn-mixed-purification-original-unit-coefficients}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelGlobalCanonicalTrace.mixedPurificationMap_over_unit}
\uses{lem:kn-global-trace-actual-purification,lem:kn-global-trace-mixed-extended-counit}
\leanok
The original map satisfies
$\rho\circ(S\to C)=S\to P$ as an equality of entire ring homomorphisms.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every $s\in S$, the value of $\rho$ is the value of the original
$S$-algebra equivalence.  That equivalence sends the image of $s$ to
the same structural image of $s$ in $P$.  Equality on every coefficient
gives equality of the ring homomorphisms.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The entire original base square commutes]
\label{lem:kn-mixed-purification-original-ring-square}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelGlobalCanonicalTrace.mixedPurification_ring_square}
\uses{lem:kn-mixed-purification-original-unit-coefficients}
\leanok
The original structural maps satisfy
\[
 \rho\circ(B\to C)\circ(A\to B)=(S\to P)\circ(A\to S).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two maps from $A$ into $S\otimes_A B$ are equal by the balancing
relation of the tensor product.  Compose this equality with the same
original $\rho$.  Its value on the image of $S$ is the original
structural map $S\to P$ by the preceding lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The original whole spectrum square commutes]
\label{lem:kn-mixed-purification-original-scheme-square}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelGlobalCanonicalTrace.mixedPurification_scheme_square}
\uses{lem:kn-mixed-purification-original-ring-square}
\leanok
The original spectrum maps form a commuting square
\[
 \operatorname{Spec}P\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}B
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A
 \;=\;
 \operatorname{Spec}P\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}S
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A.
\]
The map to $\operatorname{Spec}B$ is the original purification followed
by the original tensor-right projection, on the entire original schemes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the contravariant spectrum functor to the equality of entire
ring homomorphisms.  Its composition law identifies the resulting maps
with the displayed original composites.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Original field-structure triangles]
\label{lem:kn-mixed-purification-original-field-triangles}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelGlobalCanonicalTrace.mixedBase_structure_triangle,EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelGlobalCanonicalTrace.mixedPure_structure_triangle}
\uses{lem:kn-global-trace-actual-purification}
\leanok
The original maps $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ and
$\operatorname{Spec}P\to\operatorname{Spec}S$ preserve their original
maps to $\operatorname{Spec}k$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each original ring map is the indicated map of $k$-algebras.  Its
coefficient identity on $k$ gives the structural triangle after applying
the spectrum functor.  The mixed structural map is its defining original
map induced by $k\to B$.
\end{proof}

These are ordinary algebra and scheme identities.  They do not assert a
canonical-section commuting square, a counit normalization, a canonical
trace, or a vanishing theorem.
