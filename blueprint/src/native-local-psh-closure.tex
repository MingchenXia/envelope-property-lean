\uses{scheme:actual-native-convex-pl-closure,scheme:actual-native-unit-convex-pl-continuity,analysis:actual-ereal-antitone-infima,analysis:actual-rational-antitone-real-approximation}
Actual decreasing convex PL limits, and intrinsic locally psh functions, are
stable under finite maxima, finite sums, nonnegative rational multiples and
all finite REAL translates. They restrict to true open subsets and preserve
original domainwise equality; the intrinsic local class is genuinely local
on its actual point space. Negative infinity remains allowed. No uniform
convergence, common countable presentation neighborhood or classically
subharmonicity assumption is introduced.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For decreasing limits use the original termwise maximum or sum of the actual
sequences and the preceding genuine convex PL closure. Their terms remain
antitone. Pure extended-real antitone-infimum identities identify their
ORIGINAL limits. For sums, each first convex PL value is finite, so neither
original infimum is positive infinity; the actual mixed-infinity obstruction
is excluded by the proved no-positive-infinity bounds. Strictly positive
rational scaling commutes with actual infima; at factor zero use the genuine
constant-zero sequence.

For a real translate $c$, take the ACTUAL antitone rational sequence $q_j>c$
with original infimum $c$. Add $q_j$ to the original $j$th convex PL term.
Both original sequences are antitone and the terms are finite. The true
extended-real sum identity gives precisely the ORIGINAL limit $u+c$,
including where $u$ is negative infinity. Every local-presentation statement
is obtained on actual finite intersections of the supplied open neighborhoods;
the genuine sequences are restricted there. No countable intersection is
used. Domainwise equality and locality follow directly from the actual local
neighborhood and original infimum definitions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Closure-controlled genuine piecewise maximum]
\label{scheme:actual-native-local-psh-controlled-gluing}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.IsConvexPLOn.piecewise_max}
\uses{scheme:actual-native-convex-pl-closure,scheme:actual-native-local-psh-closure}
Let $U'\subseteq U$ be actual open native subsets, with $u$ intrinsically locally
psh on $U$ and $v$ intrinsically locally psh on $U'$. If
\[
 \overline{\{x\in U':u(x)<v(x)\}}\cap U\ \subseteq U',
\]
then the ORIGINAL function equal to $\max(u,v)$ on $U'$ and equal to $u$ outside
$U'$ is intrinsically locally psh on $U$. The same conclusion holds for
convex PL functions when both original functions are convex PL.
There is no continuity or function-class assertion outside the stated domain.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a point of $U'$ use that actual open neighborhood and the genuine binary
maximum theorem after restricting the original $u$. At a point of
$U\setminus U'$, the closure condition puts the point outside the ACTUAL
closure of the strict-inequality locus. Its genuine complement, intersected
with the original open $U$, is a true neighborhood. At every point there that
lies in $U'$, failure of the strict inequality gives $v\leq u$, so the ORIGINAL
piecewise maximum equals $u$. Outside $U'$ the same equality is its definition.
Thus the entire original function agrees with $u$ on this neighborhood.
Domainwise congruence, genuine open restriction and actual locality prove the
claim. No boundary value, outside continuity or analytic gluing certificate is
supplied.
\end{proof}
