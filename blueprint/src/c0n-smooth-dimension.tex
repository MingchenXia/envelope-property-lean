\section{The original smooth integral scheme has a produced pure dimension}
\label{sec:c0n-smooth-dimension}

The native relative-dimension constancy theorem already proves the needed
mathematics. This continuation reuses that actual theorem on the SAME
original scheme and structure map. No dimension or pure-dimension
certificate is an additional input. Global finite type or projectivity is
not needed for this bridge.

\begin{lemma}[Original smooth integral geometry internally gives one relative dimension]
\label{lem:c0n-smooth-integral-pure-dimension}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.SmoothPureDimension.exists_smoothOfRelativeDimension,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.SmoothPureDimension.exists_smoothOfRelativeDimension_charZero}
Let $k$ be a perfect field, $X$ the original integral scheme, and
$p:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ the original smooth structure map. Then
\[
 \exists n\in\mathbb N,\quad
 \operatorname{SmoothOfRelativeDimension}(n,p).
\]
In particular this conclusion follows from the original characteristic-zero
field and the same original integral smooth scheme, without a new
perfectness or dimension hypothesis.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Integral schemes have nonempty irreducible underlying spaces. Irreducibility
gives preconnectedness. Apply the EXISTING native theorem
\nolinkurl{exists_smoothOfRelativeDimension_of_preconnectedSpace} to the
SAME original $p$.

Its actual proof chooses an original standard-smooth affine neighbourhood
at a point. Its genuine presentation gives a finite relative dimension
$n$. The native dimension count
$x\mapsto\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}+\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(x)$ is locally
constant by actual standard-smooth affine charts and the native smooth
local-ring dimension formula. Preconnectedness makes this SAME count
constant. Every actual original standard-smooth chart therefore has the
chosen dimension $n$, and the native source-local smooth-dimension theorem
gives the conclusion for the original whole scheme and original map.
All this mathematics is imported from the proved native theorem, rather
than replaced by a dimension certificate. In characteristic zero the
original field already has its native perfect-field instance.
\end{proof}

\label{def:c0n-produced-smooth-dimension}

$\operatorname{dimension}(p)$ from the preceding proved existence.
Then the SAME original structure map actually satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{SmoothOfRelativeDimension}(\operatorname{dimension}(p),p).
\]
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
Take the natural witness from the preceding actual existence theorem and
use that theorem's proof of its native relative-dimension property. No
new global instance, assumed witness, or expected pure-dimension property
is introduced. Consumers may either destruct the existence theorem or use

actual original scheme or actual polynomial family.
\uses{lem:c0n-smooth-integral-pure-dimension}
\end{proof}
