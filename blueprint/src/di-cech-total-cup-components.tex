\section{Original bidegree inputs recover the signed cup}

\begin{theorem}[The whole original pure-bidegree input formula]
\label{thm:di-cech-total-cup-pure-input}
\uses{def:di-cech-total-cup}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.DI.CechTotalCupComponents.totalCup_ιTotal}
Let $q+j=a$ and $r+j'=b$. Compose the actual total cup
$T^a\otimes T^b\to T^{a+b}$ with the tensor map of the original total
inclusions from bidegrees $(q,j)$ and $(r,j')$. The entire resulting
sheaf morphism is $(-1)^{jr}$ times the original ordered cup followed
by the original inclusion of bidegree $(r+q,j+j')$ into total degree
$a+b$. This is an equality of actual morphisms, with no differential,
closedness, or leading-homology hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original tensor map preserves composition, as is seen on every
original tensor section using the sheafification unit's naturality.
Precomposing each summand of the total cup thus gives the tensor map
of the two original inclusion--projection composites, followed by its
ordered cup and total inclusion. Native total inclusion--projection
orthogonality makes a summand zero unless both antidiagonal indices
are the specified input bidegrees. A zero component gives a zero
tensor map, by additivity of the actual tensor functor in that input.
For the unique remaining pair, both inclusion--projection maps are
identities, and their tensor map is the identity on the original
tensor sheaf. The finite sum therefore reduces to precisely the
stated signed ordered cup and original total inclusion. Proof
irrelevance identifies the original arithmetic equality witnesses.
\end{proof}
