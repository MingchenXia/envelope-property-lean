\section{Affine geometric relative Frobenius and quasicoherent complex terms}

\begin{theorem}[Affineness of the original geometric relative Frobenius]
\label{thm:cartier-geometric-relative-frobenius-affine}
\leanok
\uses{thm:cartier-original-entire-scheme-relative-frobenius,thm:cartier-original-frobenius-affine-pushforward}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Cartier.GeometricRelativeFrobeniusQuasicoherent.isAffineHom_map}
For the original $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ in prime characteristic
$p$, its actual relative Frobenius $F:X\to X^{(p)}$ is affine.
No smoothness, perfection, nonemptiness or affine-comparison
certificate is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original base field Frobenius is a morphism between affine
schemes, hence affine. Its base change $\pi_1:X^{(p)}\to X$ is
therefore affine. The whole original projection identity says
$\pi_1\circ F=F_X$, where $F_X$ is the original absolute Frobenius.
The latter is affine by the proved original affine-Frobenius theorem.
For an affine morphism $g$, a composite $g\circ h$ is affine exactly
when $h$ is affine. Apply this cancellation property to $g=\pi_1$
and $h=F$. It yields affineness of the same actual relative
Frobenius, without replacing its source, target or section maps.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quasicoherence of every original geometric complex term]
\label{thm:cartier-geometric-relative-complex-quasicoherent}
\leanok
\uses{thm:cartier-geometric-relative-frobenius-affine,thm:smooth-original-exterior-quasicoherent,def:cartier-geometric-chart-section-complex}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Cartier.GeometricRelativeFrobeniusQuasicoherent.complex_term_isQuasicoherent}
If the original $f$ is smooth of relative dimension $n$, every term
of the original global geometric relative complex
$K=F_*\Omega^\bullet_{X/k}$ is quasicoherent as a module on the
same actual $X^{(p)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In degree $q$, the actual original term is precisely
$F_*\Omega^q_{X/k}$ by the definition of the global complex.
The original smooth exterior-cotangent quasicoherence theorem applies
in every degree, including degree zero. The preceding theorem makes
the same original $F$ affine. Pushforward of a quasicoherent module
along an affine scheme morphism is quasicoherent. Apply this theorem
to the actual original exterior module and the actual original
relative Frobenius. Its output is exactly the stated term of $K$.
\end{proof}

Affineness and termwise quasicoherence supply the geometric hypotheses
for an affine comparison with associated module complexes. They do
not themselves identify homology of sections with sections of the
global homology sheaf or construct a global Cartier isomorphism.
