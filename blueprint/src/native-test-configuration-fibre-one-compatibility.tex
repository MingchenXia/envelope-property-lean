\section{The literal source fibre-one identification is preserved}
\label{sec:native-test-configuration-fibre-one-compatibility}

\begin{theorem}[Whole original fibre-one compatibility {\cite[\S1.2]{BJ22}}]
\label{thm:native-test-configuration-fibre-one-compatibility}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.NativeTestConfiguration.TestConfiguration.fibreOneUnitOpenInclusion, EnvelopeProperty.NativeTestConfiguration.TestConfiguration.fibreOneUnitOpenInclusion_inclusion, EnvelopeProperty.NativeTestConfiguration.TestConfiguration.fibreOneUnitOpenInclusion_trivialization, EnvelopeProperty.NativeTestConfiguration.TestConfiguration.fibreOneUnitOpenInclusion_inverse}
\leanok
\uses{thm:native-test-configuration-unit-fibre, thm:equivariant-group-fibre-inclusion}
For every literal original test configuration, the entire
original noncentral-open trivialization and its inverse
restrict to precisely the original source fibre-one
identification $\mathcal X_1\simeq X$, with the original
group unit. The original entire fibre inclusion into the
original total model is retained as well. No fibre-restriction
or pointwise compatibility certificate is supplied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The actual lift of the original fibre $F=\mathcal X_1$ into
the same native Laurent pullback, followed by the whole
original open isomorphism, gives a morphism $F\to\mathcal X\setminus
\mathcal X_0$. Its composite with the original unit-open
inclusion is precisely the original $F\to\mathcal X$,
by the two original whole projection equations.
The general actual group/unit-fibre inclusion theorem
shows that $F\to P\simeq\mathbf G_m\times F$ is exactly
the original group unit times the identity of that same $F$.
Compose with the actual original source isomorphism
$F\simeq X$ and the original product symmetry. The whole
map becomes precisely the pair consisting of that same
source fibre-one identification and the original group unit,
as required. Composing with the actual inverse trivialization
and its whole inverse law gives the stated inverse compatibility.
Every equation is an equality of entire original morphisms.
\end{proof}
