\section{Canonical homology comparison for left exact functors}
\label{sec:left-exact-homology-comparison}

Let $S=(S_1\to S_2\to S_3)$ be a short complex in an abelian
category and let $F$ be a zero-preserving functor which preserves
finite limits. Only preservation of kernels, not right exactness,
is needed in the construction below.

\begin{lemma}[The actual cycle comparison]
\label{lem:left-exact-original-cycle-comparison}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.cyclesIso,EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.cyclesIso_hom_iCycles,EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.toCycles_comparison}
There is a canonical isomorphism
$Z(FS)\simeq F(Z(S))$. Its composite with $F(Z(S)\to S_2)$
is the actual inclusion of $Z(FS)$, and it carries the actual
boundary map of $FS$ to the image under $F$ of the boundary map
of $S$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The image under $F$ of the kernel fork defining $Z(S)$ is still
a kernel fork, because $F$ preserves finite limits and zero
morphisms. The uniqueness isomorphism between this fork and
the chosen kernel fork defining $Z(FS)$ gives the comparison.
Its inclusion identity is the defining identity of the unique
map between limiting forks. Both boundary maps become $F(S_1
\to S_2)$ after composition with this monomorphism, so the
same uniqueness proves their equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Canonical comparison without an exactness hypothesis]
\label{prop:left-exact-original-homology-comparison}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.map,EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.homologyπ_map}
There is a canonical map $H(FS)\to F(H(S))$. For every cycle,
this map first applies the preceding cycle comparison and then
the image under $F$ of the actual original homology projection.
No assertion that this comparison is an isomorphism is made.
\uses{lem:left-exact-original-cycle-comparison}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Compose $Z(FS)\simeq F(Z(S))$ with the image of the original
projection $Z(S)\to H(S)$. The original boundary map followed
by this projection is zero. Applying $F$ preserves that equality
and zero, and the boundary identity in the preceding lemma
therefore shows that the composite kills every boundary of
$FS$. The cokernel property of the actual homology projection
gives a unique map out of $H(FS)$. The same property proves
the stated formula on all cycles.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Naturality for original complex maps and functor maps]
\label{prop:left-exact-original-homology-comparison-naturality}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.naturality,EnvelopeProperty.LeftExactHomologyComparison.natTrans_naturality}
The comparison commutes with every original short-complex
morphism. For every natural transformation between two
zero-preserving finite-limit-preserving functors, it also
commutes with the actual induced short-complex map and the
component of the natural transformation on original homology.
\uses{prop:left-exact-original-homology-comparison}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For complex morphisms the two candidate cycle maps agree after
composition with the image of the target cycle inclusion, by
the defining naturality square on middle terms. That inclusion
is a monomorphism, so they agree. For a natural transformation
the same cancellation uses its naturality square on the
original cycle inclusion. Compose these equalities with the
target original homology projection and use its naturality
square. The actual homology projection of the source complex
is an epimorphism; cancellation of this epimorphism proves
both claimed homology squares.
\end{proof}

This comparison is intended for actual section functors in the
global Cartier descent. Its construction neither assumes exact
global sections nor proves that all affine section comparisons
are isomorphisms. That further assertion must be derived from
actual quasicoherent affine geometry.
