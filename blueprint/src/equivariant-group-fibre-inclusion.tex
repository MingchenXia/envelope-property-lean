\section{The whole original unit-fibre compatibility}
\label{sec:equivariant-group-fibre-inclusion}

\begin{theorem}[Restriction to precisely the original fibre {\cite[\S1.2]{BJ22}}]
\label{thm:equivariant-group-fibre-inclusion}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreInclusion.inclusion_toFibre, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreInclusion.inclusion_iso, EnvelopeProperty.EquivariantGroupFibreInclusion.unit_iso_inv}
\leanok
\uses{thm:equivariant-group-fibre-trivialization}
The genuine whole original trivialization $Z\simeq G\times F$
of an equivariant map to its regular group retains precisely
the original unit-fibre inclusion: composing the original
$F\to Z$ with the isomorphism gives the original group unit
and the identity of the same actual $F$. The entire inverse
restricts to exactly the original $F\to Z$ as well.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the same original fibre, $p=1$. The actual inverse-parameter
normalization $z\mapsto p(z)^{-1}z$ is therefore the original
identity there, by the genuine group unit action law.
The original fibre inclusion is monomorphic as a base
change of the monomorphic group unit. The defining whole
fibre-component equation followed by this inclusion now
shows that the original fibre inclusion followed by the
derived fibre component is precisely the whole identity
of $F$. Its parameter component is the original unit by
the original Cartesian fibre equation. The genuine whole
product uniqueness then gives the claimed inclusion equation.
Compose with the actual inverse isomorphism and use its
whole inverse law to obtain the corresponding inverse
restriction. No fibre-compatibility certificate is assumed.
\end{proof}
