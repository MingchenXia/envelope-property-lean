\begin{lemma}[The actual homogeneous section has its original whole scalar morphism]
\label{lem:resume-original-homogeneous-whole-scalar}
\uses{lem:fc-original-homogeneous-form-frame-coefficient,lem:fc-original-local-section-scalar,lem:scheme-original-global-section-hom,lem:blowup-projective-pulled-forms}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.BlowupHomogeneousTautologicalLine.homogeneous_localScalar}
\leanok
Let $R$ be any commutative ring, let $F\in R[X_0,\ldots,X_n]$ be
homogeneous of its original degree $d\in\mathbb N$, and let $U_i=D_+(X_i)$
be its original native projective coordinate open.  Pull back the existing
whole section morphism of $F$ in the native $\mathcal O(d)$, with pivot
$0$, along $U_i\hookrightarrow\mathbf P_R^n$.  Composing with the
existing genuine native $X_i^d$ frame is exactly the whole structure-line
endomorphism given by the original entire homogeneous coefficient
$F/X_i^d$, transported by the original affine top-section isomorphism.
The native positive-power line, frame, scheme sections and scalar actions
are retained.  No coefficient comparison is an input.  The formula includes
zero forms and degree zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $M$ for that same native positive-power line, $e_i$ for its existing
restriction frame and $s_F$ for its existing whole homogeneous section.
The original homogeneous-section frame theorem computes the coefficient
of $s_F$ in the corresponding genuine over-site frame as exactly the
entire original homogeneous fraction $F/X_i^d$.  The existing comparison
between the restriction frame and its over-site frame therefore gives the
same value for the original restriction-frame coefficient.

Apply the original whole local-section scalar theorem to $M,e_i,s_F$.
It identifies the original section morphism pulled to $U_i$, followed by
the actual pullback of $e_i$, with multiplication by the affine top-section
image of precisely that original restriction-frame coefficient.  Substitute
the coefficient equality just proved.  The section morphism here is
definitionally the existing original homogeneous section morphism; the
frame is the same existing native positive-power trivialization.  Thus the
equality is an equality of whole original module morphisms with their same
actions.  Neither step divides by the form or assumes positive degree, so
the same argument covers $F=0$ and $d=0$.
\end{proof}
