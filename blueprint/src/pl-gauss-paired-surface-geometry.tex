\section{The original central curve inside an actual regular altered pair}

Let $k$ be a field, put $B=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, and write
$\epsilon:\operatorname{Spec}k\to B$ and $\gamma:B\to\operatorname{Spec}k$
for the original evaluation-at-zero and coefficient morphisms. Thus
$\epsilon\gg\gamma=\operatorname{id}$. Let $N$ be integral, with
an actual projective dominant $b:N\to B$ and $\dim N=2$. Let
$C_1$ be integral and projective over $k$, with actual structure $q_1$,
$\dim C_1=1$, and an actual closed immersion $c_1:C_1\hookrightarrow N$
such that $c_1\gg b=q_1\gg\epsilon$.

Put $p_N=b\gg\gamma$ and $Z=b^{-1}V(t)$. An actual altered pair for
$(N,p_N,Z)$ consists here of the original alteration $a:R\to N$,
open immersion $e:R\hookrightarrow\overline R$, their field structures,
regular integral projective $\overline R$, and an actual SNC ideal
sheaf datum $D$ on $\overline R$. Its whole structure triangles are
retained and its actual support satisfies
\[
 |D|=e(a^{-1}Z)\cup(\operatorname{im}e)^c.
\]
The following are geometric consumers of this entire actual pair data.
They do not assert that pair data is an external existence input: the
unconditional original-model theorem constructs it by the already proved
alteration theorem. No selected component, central curve above $C_1$,
local equation or parameter system is part of the pair input.

\begin{lemma}[The selected actual SNC component stays in the open source]
\label{lem:pl-gauss-paired-data-central-component}
\uses{def:alteration-output,def:variety,def:projective-over,def:snc,lem:divisor-components,cor:proper-central-fiber-closed-compactification-image,lem:sm-dj-dim-alteration,lem:sm-dj-dim-finite,cor:function-field-dimension-dense-open,lem:actual-cartier-component-dimension,thm:proper-flattening-dedekind-native-flat,lem:regular-open,thm:snc-regular-component-cartier}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.PLGaussPairedSurfaceGeometry.exists_component_lift_through_central_curve, EnvelopeProperty.PLGaussPairedSurfaceGeometry.exists_regular_component_through_central_curve}
\leanok
For the actual pair just described there is an actual native support
component $E$ of $D$ such that
\[
 E\subseteq e(a^{-1}Z)\subseteq\operatorname{im}e.
\]
Let $C_2$ be the literal reduced vanishing-ideal closed scheme of this
same $E$, with original $\iota_E:C_2\hookrightarrow\overline R$.
There is an actual closed immersion $j:C_2\hookrightarrow R$ with
$j\gg e=\iota_E$, and an actual $w\in C_2$ such that
$(j\gg a)(w)=c_1(\eta_{C_1})$.
The actual schemes $R$ and $\overline R$ are regular integral surfaces,
$C_2$ is a regular integral projective curve of dimension one, and
$b_R=a\gg b$ is proper, dominant and flat over the same $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Properness of $a$ and projectivity of $b$ make $b_R$ proper. Its base
change along $\epsilon$ is proper over $k$. The pair's whole field
triangles give
$e\gg p_{\overline R}=b_R\gg\gamma$. The proper-central-fibre
producer therefore proves that the entire actual subset
$H=e(b_R^{-1}V(t))=e(a^{-1}Z)$ is closed in $\overline R$.
Here $\operatorname{im}\epsilon=V(t)$ follows from the actual kernel
$(t)$ of evaluation at zero. No absolute properness of $R$ is used.

Surjectivity of the original alteration chooses $y\in R$ with
$a(y)=c_1(\eta_{C_1})$. The actual central triangle implies
$b_R(y)\in V(t)$, hence $e(y)\in H$. Choose the actual irreducible
component of the support space $|D|$ through this very point, and let
$E$ be its native ambient closed image. This is a genuine support
component, containing $e(y)$ and contained in $|D|$.
Both $H$ and $(\operatorname{im}e)^c$ are closed. Their union contains
$E$, so irreducibility forces $E$ into one of them. The second choice
is impossible because $e(y)\in E\cap\operatorname{im}e$.
Thus $E\subseteq H\subseteq\operatorname{im}e$.

The actual open-immersion lifting property gives $j$ on the literal
reduced closed component $C_2$, with $j\gg e=\iota_E$. The composite
is a closed immersion and $e$ is separated, so closed-immersion
postcomposition cancellation makes the same $j$ closed. A point $w$
of the original reduced component above $e(y)$ exists because its
closed immersion has precisely image $E$. Injectivity of $e$ and
the whole factorization give $j(w)=y$, and hence the asserted actual
point above $c_1(\eta_{C_1})$.

The alteration preserves the whole finite-type field dimension,
giving $\dim R=\dim N=2$. The actual dense open inclusion $e$ into
the integral field variety $\overline R$ preserves dimension, so
$\dim\overline R=2$. The SNC datum is Cartier; the actual Cartier
component dimension formula gives
$\dim C_2+1=\dim\overline R=2$. The finite-type field dimension of
$C_2$ is a natural number, and this equality forces $\dim C_2=1$.
The native reduced support component is integral. Singleton SNC
intersection regularity applies to its literal vanishing-ideal
scheme, making $C_2$ regular. Closedness of $\iota_E$ and the actual
projective embedding of $\overline R$ make $C_2$ projective over $k$.
Regularity of $R$ is inherited through the original open immersion.
The original dominant alteration followed by dominant $b$ makes
$b_R$ dominant. Since $R$ is integral and $k[t]$ is Dedekind, the
proved native dominant-to-Dedekind theorem makes this same $b_R$
flat. No dimension, properness, flatness or component certificate
was supplied beyond the ordinary original geometry and actual pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The same selected regular curve maps finitely and dominantly to the original curve]
\label{thm:pl-gauss-paired-data-central-curve-finite}
\uses{lem:pl-gauss-paired-data-central-component,lem:pl-gauss-original-native-polynomial-structure-whole-equality,def:pl-gauss-original-homogeneous-cut,lem:pl-gauss-original-surface-central-curve-component,lem:pl-gauss-original-coefficient-cut-support,lem:pl-gauss-reduced-original-image-kernel,thm:curve-proper-dominant-finite,thm:fc-curve-finite-map-projective,lem:pl-gauss-original-native-structure-projective-factorization}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.PLGaussPairedSurfaceGeometry.exists_regular_curve_over_central_curve}
\leanok
From the same actual pair and ordinary $N,b,C_1,q_1,c_1$ data one
can internally choose the preceding actual $E,C_2,j$ and an actual
finite dominant $g:C_2\to C_1$ such that the whole maps satisfy
\[
 j\gg a=g\gg c_1,\qquad
 j\gg b_R=(g\gg q_1)\gg\epsilon,\qquad
 \iota_E\gg p_{\overline R}=g\gg q_1.
\]
The same original field morphism $g\gg q_1$ has an actual closed
projective factorization. The target $C_1$ is not required to be
normal, and $g$ is not assumed flat.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the preceding component internally. Extract an actual relative
projective embedding of $N$ from the given projective factorization
of $b$; its native structure is the same original $b$. Let $K_0$ be
its literal original parameter cut. The already proved original
surface component theorem supplies the actual closed
$u:C_1\hookrightarrow K_0$ with $u\gg\iota_0=c_1$ and
$\operatorname{im}u$ a genuine irreducible component of $K_0$.

Since $E\subseteq H$ and $e$ is injective, the entire image of
$j\gg a$ lies in $Z=|K_0|$. Reducedness of $C_2$ identifies the
whole image kernel with the vanishing ideal of its closed image.
The support inclusion therefore gives the actual ideal inclusion
needed to lift through $\iota_0$. This constructs
$v:C_2\to K_0$ with $v\gg\iota_0=j\gg a$.
The point $w$ above the original generic curve point satisfies
$v(w)=u(\eta_{C_1})$, by injectivity of $\iota_0$.
The closure of $\operatorname{im}v$ is irreducible. It contains
$\overline{\{u(\eta_{C_1})\}}=\operatorname{im}u$, since $u$ is
closed and $C_1$ is integral. Maximality of this genuine component
of $K_0$ forces the whole closure into $\operatorname{im}u$.
Thus the entire actual image of $v$ is contained in the image of $u$.
Reduced-source image-kernel equality gives the actual closed-lift
ideal inclusion. Lifting through the same $u$ gives $g$, and cancelling
$\iota_0$ in its whole factorization proves $j\gg a=g\gg c_1$.

The same point satisfies $g(w)=\eta_{C_1}$, by injectivity of $u$.
Its image therefore contains the generic point and is dense, so $g$
is dominant. The actual composite $j\gg a$ is proper. It equals
$g\gg c_1$, and $c_1$ is separated, hence proper postcomposition
cancellation makes $g$ proper. The original source $C_2$ is
Noetherian and has dimension one. The target has dimension one
and is nontrivial: otherwise its integral space has a single point
and dimension zero. The proved proper-dominant integral-curve
theorem now makes this actual $g$ finite without target normality.
The existing finite-composite projective-factorization producer
applied to this $g$ and the original $q_1$ proves the asserted
projectivity of the same $g\gg q_1$.

Compose the whole model triangle with $b$ and substitute the
original central triangle to obtain the second equality. Composing
it with $\gamma$ gives $j\gg a\gg p_N=g\gg q_1$.
The pair's actual field triangles and $j\gg e=\iota_E$ identify
the left side with $\iota_E\gg p_{\overline R}$, proving the
third whole equality. No field structure or original map is replaced.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual Cartier and curve parameters at every nongeneric curve point]
\label{thm:pl-gauss-paired-data-local-parameters}
\uses{thm:pl-gauss-paired-data-central-curve-finite,def:snc,thm:snc-regular-component-cartier,lem:node-fitting-ideal-sheaf-stalks,lem:node-fitting-ideal-sheaf-stalk-kernel,thm:node-fitting-ideal-sheaf-actual-stalk-quotient,thm:fc-curve-proper-support-points,def:regular-scheme,alggeom:original-stalk-coefficient-map,alggeom:original-stalk-coefficient-triangle,alggeom:original-stalk-coefficient-injective,lem:scheme-stalk-coefficients-naturality}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.PLGaussPairedSurfaceGeometry.exists_regular_paired_surface_parameters}
\notready
The same pair and ordinary original surface and curve data internally
give $E,C_2,j,g$ as above, and the following holds at every nongeneric
$x\in C_2$. Put
\[
 y=j(x),\quad S=\mathcal O_{R,y},\quad Q=\mathcal O_{C_2,x},\quad
 \psi=j_x^*:S\to Q,\quad
 \rho:k[t]\to S,\quad t_S=\rho(t),
\]
where $\rho$ is the literal original stalk coefficient map of $b_R$.
There are actual $\pi,z\in S$ such that
\[
 \ker\psi=(\pi),\qquad \mathfrak m_S=(\pi,z),\qquad
 \pi\text{ is prime},\qquad \pi\nmid z,\qquad
 t_S\ne0,\qquad \pi\mid t_S.
\]
The same $\psi$ is surjective and induces the actual quotient
$S/(\pi)\simeq Q$. The original ring $Q$ is a discrete valuation
ring. Intersections with other boundary components and their
multiplicities are retained.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the preceding curve internally and fix $x$. Write
$\overline x=\iota_E(x)=e(j(x))$. The proved SNC component Cartier
theorem makes the actual vanishing ideal of $E$ effective Cartier.
Choose one of its original Cartier affine neighbourhoods at
$\overline x$, with actual regular section $f$ generating the
entire component ideal. The neighbourhood is nonempty and its
section ring is a domain, since $\overline R$ is integral.
Thus $f\ne0$. Its germ $\overline\pi$ is nonzero by original
integral-scheme germ injectivity. The whole stalk-ideal computation
on this very affine neighbourhood gives
$I_{E,\overline x}=(\overline\pi)$.

The actual open inclusion induces a ring isomorphism
$\mathcal O_{\overline R,e(y)}\simeq S$. Use the actual stalk
congruence for $j\gg e=\iota_E$ to identify its original domain
point with $\overline x$, and transport $\overline\pi$ through
these very ring isomorphisms to $\pi\in S$. The whole stalk-map
composition and this congruence preserve the same maps into $Q$.
The actual closed-subscheme kernel theorem therefore gives
$\ker\psi=(\pi)$; the original stalk quotient identifies
$S/(\pi)$ with $Q$, carrying each original class to its image
under this same $\psi$. Injectivity of the transported isomorphisms
gives $\pi\ne0$. The ring $Q$ is a domain, since $C_2$ is integral,
so this kernel is prime. A nonzero generator of a prime principal
ideal is prime, proving the required property of the actual $\pi$.

The nongeneric point of an integral dimension-one curve has actual
coheight one. The original stalk-dimension formula gives
$\dim Q=1$. Singleton SNC regularity makes this same $Q$ a regular
Noetherian local ring. Its cotangent space therefore has dimension
one. The Noetherian local-domain discrete-valuation equivalences
make $Q$ a principal ideal domain. It is not a field, since its
dimension is one, so it is a discrete valuation ring. Choose an
actual nonzero uniformizer $\overline z$ with
$\mathfrak m_Q=(\overline z)$.
The actual closed immersion $j$ makes $\psi$ surjective; lift
$\overline z$ to $z\in S$. This actual local map satisfies
$\psi^{-1}(\mathfrak m_Q)=\mathfrak m_S$. Surjective ideal
map/comap gives
\[
 \mathfrak m_S=\psi^{-1}((\psi(z)))=(z)+\ker\psi=(\pi,z).
\]
Because $\psi(z)=\overline z\ne0$, the element $z$ is not in
$(\pi)$, giving $\pi\nmid z$.

The actual integral source $R$ has proper dominant coefficient
structure $b_R$. The proved original stalk-coefficient injectivity
theorem applies to the reduced ring $k[t]$, so its original nonzero
parameter maps to $t_S\ne0$. The already proved actual whole
central triangle and stalk-coefficient naturality give
$\psi(t_S)=0$, because evaluation at zero kills $t$.
Thus $t_S\in\ker\psi=(\pi)$ and $\pi\mid t_S$.
This uses a genuine Cartier component equation and the original
curve uniformizer; it neither assumes a full adapted parameter
system nor requires $t_S$ to be a unit times a power of $\pi$.
\end{proof}
