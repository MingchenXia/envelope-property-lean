\section{The true codimension-one locus in an original integral surface}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:surface-nonclosed-nonzero-prime-height}
In every original domain of Krull dimension at most two, every nonzero nonmaximal prime has height exactly one.
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SurfacePrimeHeight.height_eq_one}\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
View the actual prime in the original prime spectrum. Its height plus coheight is at most the whole original Krull dimension, hence at most two. Nonmaximality gives positive coheight, so its height is at most one. In a domain the only height-zero prime is zero, so its nonzero height is at least one. Thus its actual height is exactly one. No catenarity, normality or Noetherian hypothesis is needed for this dimension-two argument.
\end{proof}
