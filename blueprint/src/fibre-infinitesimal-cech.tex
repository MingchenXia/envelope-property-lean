\section{The original infinitesimal ordered Cech exact sequence}

\begin{theorem}[The genuine infinitesimal sequence on an original affine family]
\label{thm:fibre-infinitesimal-ordered-cech-short-exact}
\uses{cor:fibre-infinitesimal-affine-module-short-exact}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.complexSequence,EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.complexSequence_shortExact}
\leanok
Let $X$ be a separated scheme over $\operatorname{Spec}R$ and let
$(U_i)_{i\in A}$ be any linearly ordered family of affine opens. For
every $n\ge0$, applying the ordered Cech complex on this same family to
\[
 (I\mathcal O_X)^n\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X
 \longrightarrow q_{n*}\mathcal O_{X_n}
\]
gives a short exact sequence of complexes of
$\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}R,\mathcal O)$-modules, using all the original
section maps and original coefficient actions. Neither finiteness nor
covering of the family is required for this assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cochain degree $p$. Each ordered Cech factor is evaluation on an
intersection of $p+1$ original affine opens. Such an intersection is
affine because $X$ is separated. The preceding section theorem makes
each corresponding factor sequence short exact. Restricting scalars
along the actual original coefficient map changes neither its
functions nor injectivity, surjectivity or its middle kernel.

The ordered degree-$p$ term is the product of these same factors.
A product of their inclusions is injective, since equality is detected
in every coordinate. Given an element of the third product, choose a
preimage of each coordinate under its surjective second map; this
family is an element of the middle product mapping to the prescribed
one. If an element of the middle product maps to zero, every coordinate
lies in the image of its first map; choosing those preimages gives an
element of the first product with precisely the original middle
coordinates. Thus the product sequence is short exact. The canonical
product descriptions intertwine the actual Cech maps with these
coordinate maps. The original maps are morphisms of module sheaves,
so their Cech maps commute with all restriction maps, cofaces and
alternating differentials. Degreewise short exactness consequently
gives the asserted short exact sequence of complexes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The original infinitesimal long Cech cohomology sequence]
\label{cor:fibre-infinitesimal-ordered-cech-long-exact}
\uses{thm:fibre-infinitesimal-ordered-cech-short-exact}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.connecting,EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.homology_exact_left,EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.homology_exact_middle,EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.homology_exact_right}
\leanok
There are genuine connecting maps from the degree-$p$ cohomology of
the original infinitesimal pushforward Cech complex to the degree-$(p+1)$
cohomology of the original ideal-power Cech complex. Together with the
cohomology maps of the original inclusion and projection they form a
long exact sequence, exact at all three types of term.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the snake lemma to the consecutive-degree cycles and boundaries
of the established short exact sequence of complexes. The connecting
map lifts a quotient cocycle to the middle complex, takes its
differential, lifts this uniquely through the injective first map,
and takes its cohomology class. Changing the first lift alters this
cocycle by a boundary. A cocycle has zero connecting class exactly when
its lift can be corrected by an element of the first complex to become
a cocycle, proving exactness at the third cohomology term. A first-term
cocycle maps to a middle boundary exactly when the same middle
primitive projects to a quotient cocycle whose connecting class is
the original first class, proving exactness at the first term. Finally,
a middle cocycle projects to a boundary exactly when subtracting a lift
of that boundary leaves a cocycle in the first complex, proving
exactness at the middle term. These are the original induced maps of
the established complexes.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[The whole original infinitesimal model complex]
\label{lem:fibre-infinitesimal-original-model-complex}
\uses{thm:fibre-infinitesimal-ordered-cech-short-exact}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.modelComplexIso,EnvelopeProperty.FibreInfinitesimalCech.modelHomologyIso}
\leanok
The third complex in this exact sequence is canonically isomorphic to
the ordered Cech complex of the original structure module on the literal
infinitesimal model $X_n$, on the actual inverse-image family
$(q_n^{-1}U_i)$. The isomorphism retains the original coefficient
structure through $q_n$ and gives isomorphisms on cohomology in every
degree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each ordered tuple, inverse image preserves its finite intersection.
Sections of the pushforward module on that intersection are therefore
exactly sections of the original model structure module on its original
inverse image. The coefficient actions agree by the composition rule
for the two original scheme section maps. These identifications commute
with every restriction, hence with each ordered coface and its
alternating sum. They are invertible on every whole factor and product,
so give an isomorphism of the entire complexes. Applying cohomology
gives the asserted isomorphism in every degree.
\end{proof}
