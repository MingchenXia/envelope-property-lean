\subsection{Standard structural unibranchness and genuine local normalization primes}
\label{subsec:unibranch-local-normalizations}

The local-ring definitions here are the PRIMARY definitions in the
Stacks Project, More on Algebra, section ``Local irreducibility'',
\texttt{definition-unibranch}, citing EGA~IV, Chapter~0 (23.2.1).
The inspected source is \texttt{more-algebra.tex}, commit
\texttt{6d6787f1e8c7bf2e5a90aa4c068075832160e329},
lines 31124--31134. The distinct strict-henselization characterization
\texttt{lemma-geometrically-unibranch}, lines 31453ff, is NOT
asserted formalized. No definition below assumes normalization
spectrum injectivity or a singleton native fibre.

\begin{definition}[The actual reduced normalization and standard local-ring unibranchness]
\label{algebra:actual-structural-unibranch-local-ring}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.Reduced,
EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.Ring,
EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.coefficientMap,
EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.reduced_inclusion_injective,
EnvelopeProperty.IsUnibranchLocalRing,
EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.coefficientMap_isLocalHom,
EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.residueFieldMap,
EnvelopeProperty.LocalNormalization.residueFieldMap_residue,
EnvelopeProperty.IsGeometricallyUnibranchLocalRing}
For an ACTUAL local commutative ring $R$, set
$R_{\mathrm{red}}=R/\sqrt{(0)}$ and
$N(R)=\overline{R_{\mathrm{red}}}^{\,\operatorname{Frac}(R_{\mathrm{red}})}$,
the LITERAL integral closure in its actual fraction ring.
The standard unibranch property says $R_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a
domain and $N(R)$ is local. The geometric property additionally
says that the ACTUAL induced map $\kappa(R)\to\kappa(N(R))$
is purely inseparable. This residue map is constructed from the
original coefficient map; no unrelated residue-field action,
field identification or point-fibre condition is supplied.
\leanok
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
Use the genuine nilradical quotient, its true fraction ring and
existing literal integral closure. If the reduction is a domain,
its coefficient map into its normalization is injective: equality
there implies equality in its ACTUAL fraction field, whose
original coefficient map is injective.

The true quotient $R\to R_{\mathrm{red}}$ is surjective between
local rings, hence local. The actual injective integral map
$R_{\mathrm{red}}\to N(R)$ is local, by the proved integral
unit-reflection theorem. Their whole composite is therefore
local and induces its GENUINE residue-field map. The actual
residue naturality identity preserves every original element.
The additional pure-inseparability field is imposed on THIS
map, exactly as in the cited standard structural definition.
These are structural algebraic properties, not an assumed
normalization homeomorphism or fibre certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole coefficient change of integral closures]
\label{algebra:actual-same-ambient-integral-closure-map}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.closureMap,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.closureMap_coe,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.closureEquivOfIntegralBase,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.closureEquivOfIntegralBase_coe}
Let $A\to S\to K$ be an ACTUAL scalar tower of arbitrary
commutative rings. Integral coefficient change constructs a
whole $A$-algebra map
$\overline A^{\,K}\to\overline S^{\,K}$, fixing EVERY
original ambient element. If $S$ is integral over $A$, the SAME
map is a genuine algebra equivalence. No field, domain,
Noetherianity or injectivity hypothesis is required.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An element integral over $A$ is integral over $S$ by mapping
its actual monic polynomial coefficients through the original
scalar tower. Thus the ambient identity restricts to a true
algebra homomorphism of the two literal closures; all ring laws
and coefficient identities hold on EVERY element. If $S/A$
is integral, genuine transitivity proves the reverse inclusion
of integral elements. The inverse is the actual ambient identity
on those elements. Both whole inverse identities follow,
without replacing either integral closure by a chosen isomorphic
ring or assuming an equality of ideal supports.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual localization of the literal integral closure]
\label{algebra:actual-integral-closure-localization}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.isLocalization_closure,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.localizationEquiv,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.localizationEquiv_algebraMap}
\uses{algebra:actual-same-ambient-integral-closure-map}
Suppose $S$ is the ACTUAL localization of $A$ at a submonoid $M$,
and $A\to S\to K$ is the original scalar tower, still for
arbitrary commutative rings. The constructed whole closure map
makes $\overline S^{\,K}$ a GENUINE localization of
$\overline A^{\,K}$ at the image of $M$. Consequently the
literal quotient localization is canonically algebra-equivalent
to that same-ambient integral closure. Every original closure
element and every coefficient follows its ENTIRE actual map.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $m\in M$ is a true unit in $S$, so its original image
in $K$ is a unit. Thus $K$ itself is genuinely the localization
of $K$ at those original coefficient images. Construct the
closure action through the preceding ACTUAL identity-on-ambient
map; its two coefficient towers commute on EVERY element.
The checked theorem that integral closure commutes with actual
localization now applies with these CONSTRUCTED closure actions
and towers. It gives the true localization structure, not a
localization certificate supplied by the caller. Uniqueness of
actual localizations produces the displayed whole algebra
equivalence and its original-element formula. This primitive
needs neither an ambient field nor a domain or finite
normalization; the actual domain/fraction-field case is included.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[True local normalization derives original prime uniqueness]
\label{algebra:actual-local-normalization-prime-uniqueness}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.prime_map_isMaximal_of_base_localization,
EnvelopeProperty.prime_eq_of_isLocalRing_baseLocalization,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.primes_eq_of_local_normalization_isLocal}
\uses{algebra:actual-integral-closure-localization}
For an actual integral extension $A\to B$ and original prime
$p$, every genuine prime $q$ of $B$ contracting to $p$ extends
to a MAXIMAL prime after localizing $B$ at the image of
$A\setminus p$. If this ACTUAL localization is a local ring,
two original primes contracting to $p$ are equal.
In particular, localness of the LITERAL local integral closure
$\overline{A_p}^{\,K}$ derives prime uniqueness in the original
$\overline A^{\,K}$. No fibre singleton, injectivity or
residue-comparison certificate is an input.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A prime contracting to $p$ is disjoint from the original mapped
complement $A\setminus p$. Actual localization prime
correspondence therefore extends it, and contraction recovers
the ENTIRE original prime. The genuine localized coefficient
map $A_p\to B_{A\setminus p}$ is integral. The whole original
coefficient square shows the extended prime contracts to the
actual maximal ideal of $A_p$. Integral maximality then makes
the extended prime MAXIMAL. If that original localization is
local, both extended primes are its UNIQUE actual maximal ideal.
Contract through the genuine localization correspondence to get
equality of the original primes. Apply the preceding actual
closure-localization structure for the claimed literal
normalization specialization. No Noetherian, domain, finite
module or native-topology assumption enters this prime lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual fraction-field choice preserves the literal normalization]
\label{algebra:actual-normalization-fraction-field-choice}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.fractionFieldNormalizationEquiv,
EnvelopeProperty.NormalizationLocalization.fractionFieldNormalizationEquiv_algebraMap}
For ANY actual fraction ring $K$ of $A$ with its ORIGINAL
coefficient action, there is a genuine $A$-algebra equivalence
$\overline A^{\,\operatorname{Frac}A}\simeq_A\overline A^{\,K}$,
preserving every original coefficient. In the domain case these
are the genuine original fraction fields. No field identification
is supplied or made by fiat.
\leanok
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both ambient fraction rings are actual localizations at the
original non-zero-divisors. Localization uniqueness constructs
the true coefficient-preserving ambient algebra equivalence.
Its actual integral-closure functor restricts to the integral
elements; the whole inverse laws hold on EVERY ambient element,
so on every integral element. Its algebra law proves the exact
original coefficient formula.
\end{proof}
