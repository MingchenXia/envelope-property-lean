\section{Denominator ideals on the original integral scheme}

\begin{definition}[The original denominator ideal sheaf]
\label{def:fc-scheme-rational-denominators}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.affineIdeal, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.idealSheaf}
\uses{thm:fc-rational-denominator-localization}
Let $X$ be the original integral scheme and $f\in K(X)$ an arbitrary
original rational function. On a nonempty original affine open $U$, take
$D_{\Gamma(X,U)}(f)$ using the original generic-germ embedding into the
same $K(X)$. On the empty affine open take the whole zero ring ideal.
These ideals define an actual ideal sheaf on the original $X$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[True original chart and stalk identities]
\label{thm:fc-scheme-rational-denominator-stalks}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.affineIdeal_eq, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.affineIdeal_basicOpen, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.idealSheaf_ideal, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.idealSheaf_ideal_ne_bot, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.stalkIdeal_eq, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeRationalDenominators.stalkIdeal_ne_bot}
\uses{def:fc-scheme-rational-denominators}
The constructed ideal on every nonempty original affine chart is exactly
the original denominator ideal there. Its original stalk at every original
point $x$ is exactly
\[
 D_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}(f)\subseteq\mathcal O_{X,x},
\]
with the literal original stalk-to-function-field map. Every such original
chart ideal and every such original stalk is nonzero. No comparison ideal,
frame, finite denominator presentation, normality or Cartier premise is
supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restriction of the original generic germ along an original basic open
is the same generic germ. Thus the original coefficient maps form the
actual scalar tower. The true denominator-localization theorem gives
exactly the ideal-sheaf basic-open compatibility. Empty basic opens have
zero section ring, so their ideals agree as well. This constructs the
original ideal sheaf, and its affine ideals have the asserted literal
identities.

Choose an actual affine neighborhood of $x$. The original ideal's stalk
is the ideal extended by the original germ, and the actual original stalk
is the localization of that affine section ring at its actual prime.
Apply the same denominator-localization theorem along the genuine native
section--stalk--function-field tower. Finally the original integral
scheme's function field is the fraction field of each nonempty affine
section ring and of each original stalk. Genuine fraction-field
surjectivity produces their nonzero denominators.
\end{proof}
