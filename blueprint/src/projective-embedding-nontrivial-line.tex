\begin{lemma}[Trivial projective twisting forces all ideals to be zero or unit]
\label{lem:fc-trivial-projective-twist-ideals}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.ProjectiveEmbeddingNontrivialLine.ideal_eq_bot_or_top_of_trivial}
\leanok
\uses{thm:fc-original-generated-projective-ideal-zero-or-unit}
Let $X$ be an integral projective scheme over a field, and let
$i:X\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^n_k$ be a closed immersion. If
$i^*\mathcal O(1)$ is trivial, every original ideal sheaf on $X$ is zero
or the unit ideal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All positive tensor powers of the given trivialization identify the actual
pulled-back twists $i^*\mathcal O(d)$ with $\mathcal O_X$. The scheme is
locally Noetherian because it is of finite type over a field. Thus any
original ideal sheaf $I$ is a finite type quasicoherent module. Projective
Serre generation gives global generators for $I\otimes i^*\mathcal O(d)$
for some $d$. The whole twist trivialization and the tensor unitor
transport these generators to $I$ itself. The original generated-ideal
theorem now makes $I$ zero or unit.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Trivial embedding line forces dimension zero]
\label{lem:fc-trivial-embedding-line-dimension-zero}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.ProjectiveEmbeddingNontrivialLine.dimension_le_zero_of_trivial, EnvelopeProperty.ProjectiveEmbeddingNontrivialLine.nontrivial_of_isVeryAmple}
\leanok
\uses{lem:fc-trivial-projective-twist-ideals}
Under the preceding hypotheses the topological dimension of $X$ is at
most zero. Consequently the actual embedding line of any integral
positive-dimensional projective scheme is nontrivial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each original point $x$, take the original vanishing ideal of the
closed subset $\overline{\{x\}}$. Its support is exactly that closed
subset. The ideal cannot be the unit ideal since its support contains
$x$. It is therefore zero, and its support is all of $X$. Hence every
point has closure $X$. The underlying scheme space is $T_0$, so equality
of singleton closures makes all its points equal. A space with at most
one point is discrete, and every irreducible closed subset is maximal.
Its topological dimension is therefore at most zero. A positive-dimensional
scheme consequently admits no trivialization of its embedding line.
\end{proof}
