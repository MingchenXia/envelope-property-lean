\section{Actual global attainment for directed original ideal sheaves}

\begin{theorem}[A genuine directed ideal-sheaf supremum is attained]\label{thm:sc0-directed-original-ideal-sheaf-attainment}
\lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.exists_eq_iSup_of_directed}
\leanok
Let $X$ be the original quasi-compact locally Noetherian scheme.
Every nonempty directed family $(F_a)$ of actual ideal sheaves on
$X$ has its whole actual global supremum equal to an actual
constituent $F_c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compactness extracts an actual finite subcover of the original
affine open cover. Write its actual affine ranges as $U_i$.
For each $i$, the original section ring $\Gamma(X,U_i)$ is
Noetherian. The nonempty set of actual ideals $F_a(U_i)$ therefore
has a maximal member, say $F_{a_i}(U_i)$. Directedness upgrades
maximality to a greatest member: for any $a$, take a common
upper constituent $F_b$ of $F_a$ and $F_{a_i}$. Maximality forces
$F_b(U_i)=F_{a_i}(U_i)$, so $F_a(U_i)\subseteq F_{a_i}(U_i)$.

Apply directedness finitely many times to the actual indices $a_i$.
It gives an actual common constituent $F_c$ above all $F_{a_i}$.
Every $F_a$ is then below $F_c$ on every original affine range
$U_i$. The actual affine-cover ideal-sheaf order-detection theorem
gives $F_a\subseteq F_c$ globally. Thus the whole supremum is
below $F_c$, and the opposite inclusion follows because $F_c$
is one of its constituents. No maximality or finite cover was
supplied as an additional hypothesis.
\end{proof}
