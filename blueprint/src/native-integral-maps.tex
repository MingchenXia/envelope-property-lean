\subsection{Genuine integral maps of native valued scheme points}
\label{subsec:native-integral-maps}

\begin{theorem}[Properness is local on an actual open cover of the target]
\label{topology:proper-map-locality}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.isProperMap_iff_restrictPreimage_of_openCover}
\leanok
For an actually continuous map $f:Z\to W$ of arbitrary topological
spaces and any actual open cover $(U_i)$ of $W$, the map is proper if
and only if every restricted map $f^{-1}(U_i)\to U_i$ is proper.
No Hausdorff or local-compactness hypothesis is required.
Continuity of the original whole map is explicit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proper maps remain proper under restriction to a target subspace.
Conversely, properness of all restricted maps makes them closed, and
actual closed-map locality at the target gives closedness of the whole
map. For each $w\in W$, choose a genuine covering open $U_i$ containing
it. The restricted map has compact fiber over the actual subtype point
$(w,w\in U_i)$. Its continuous inclusion into the original source has
image precisely $f^{-1}\{w\}$: the subtype fiber equation implies the
whole point equation, and any original fiber point lies in that chosen
preimage because $w\in U_i$. This gives genuine compactness of every
whole fiber. Together with the explicit original continuity and proved
closedness, the actual closed-map-and-compact-fibers criterion gives
properness. The argument does not infer properness merely from compact
preimages of compact sets and uses no separation assumption.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Actual integral scheme maps induce proper native maps]
\label{scheme:integral-native-proper-map}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.SchemeValuedPoint.isProperMap_map_restrict_affine_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.SchemeValuedPoint.isProperMap_map_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.SchemeValuedPoint.isClosedMap_map_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.SchemeValuedPoint.isCompact_preimage_map_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.SchemeValuedPoint.isCompact_fibre_map_of_isIntegralHom}
\leanok
\uses{topology:proper-map-locality,spectrum:integral-proper-map,
scheme:native-closed-immersion,scheme:canonical-native-affine-atlas}
For an actual integral morphism $f:X\to Y$ of arbitrary schemes, its
original native valued-point map is proper and closed. It has compact
inverse images of compact target subsets and compact point fibers.
Neither total scheme need be separated or proper; no coefficient
seminorm is fixed or bounded. There is no additional finite-type,
noetherian, field, completeness or local-compactness hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A genuine integral scheme morphism is affine. On each actual affine
open $U$ of its target, its genuine preimage is affine, and the original
section homomorphism
$\Gamma(Y,U)\to\Gamma(X,f^{-1}U)$ is integral.
Actual native section-evaluation naturality identifies the entire
restricted native point map with seminorm pullback under that actual
ring homomorphism, conjugated by the two real affine-open
homeomorphisms. The chart identity compares all original sections,
not merely chosen generators or prime supports.

The independently constructed arbitrary-ring integral-spectrum
properness theorem proves that this actual section-spectrum pullback
is proper: its proof bounds each integral element by its real monic
equation and lifts actual convergent ultrafilters through the closed
evaluation embedding. Both genuine affine-open homeomorphisms are
proper, so conjugation makes the whole restricted native map proper.

The actual canonical affine support opens cover the target native
space. Its original native map is genuinely continuous. The preceding
proper-map locality theorem therefore proves global properness. Its
true consequences give closedness and the two compactness assertions.
An integral scheme morphism need not be finite type, so this is not an
assertion that it is algebraically proper; the conclusion concerns the
actual native point-space map.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Integral native maps above every prescribed base point]
\label{scheme:relative-integral-native-proper-map}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isProperMap_map_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isClosedMap_map_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isCompact_preimage_map_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isCompact_fibre_map_of_isIntegralHom}
\leanok
\uses{scheme:integral-native-proper-map,scheme:relative-native-closed-immersion}
For any commutative coefficient ring $R$, any prescribed real seminorm
$v$ on $R$, and any genuine commuting integral scheme morphism over
$\operatorname{Spec}R$, its native fixed-base map is proper and closed,
with compact inverse images of compact sets and compact point fibers.
No bound on $v$, proper structure morphism or separated total scheme
is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The genuine scheme diagram and the whole native composition identity
identify the source fixed-base locus with the exact preimage of the
target fixed-base locus. Restrict the proved proper absolute native map
to that target subspace and transport the source by this actual subtype
set equality. The transported function is exactly the original relative
native map on every whole point. Hence it is proper. Apply the actual
proper-map consequences for the remaining assertions. The exact
fixed-base preimage equality is the general commuting-diagram lemma;
no closed-immersion property or coefficient bound is used here.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Integral native maps across compatible prescribed coefficients]
\label{scheme:integral-native-coefficient-change}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isProperMap_mapOverBase_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isClosedMap_mapOverBase_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isCompact_preimage_mapOverBase_of_isIntegralHom,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isCompact_fibre_mapOverBase_of_isIntegralHom}
\leanok
\uses{scheme:integral-native-proper-map,scheme:native-coefficient-restriction,
scheme:relative-valued-points}
Let $\varphi:R\to S$ be a homomorphism of arbitrary commutative rings,
with compatible prescribed real seminorms $v,w$. If actual structure
maps $f_X:X\to\operatorname{Spec}S$, $f_Y:Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R$
and an actual integral morphism $g:X\to Y$ satisfy
$f_Y\circ g=\operatorname{Spec}(\varphi)\circ f_X$, then the original
whole coefficient-changing fixed-base native map is proper and closed,
with compact inverse images of compact subsets and compact point
fibers. No bound on either seminorm, properness of either structure map,
separation, or finite-type hypothesis is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual whole fixed-$w$ native locus in $X$ is closed: its original
structure map is continuous, and the native affine coefficient base is
Hausdorff by its independently proved seminorm homeomorphism. Thus its
actual subtype inclusion is a proper closed embedding, even when $X$
is nonseparated. Compose it with the proved proper absolute native map
of $g$; the resulting map into the absolute native space of $Y$ is
proper. By the genuine coefficient diagram this composition is exactly
the whole fixed-base map followed by the target subtype inclusion.
The fixed-base map is actually continuous, the target subtype inclusion
is continuous and injective, and the true proper-map cancellation
criterion therefore proves that the original fixed-base map is proper.
This injective cancellation uses the actual proper-map criterion and
requires no target Hausdorff hypothesis. Closedness and compactness of
inverse images and fibers are genuine consequences of that properness.

These are statements about actual maps and provided points. No scalar
extension point existence, integral-map surjectivity or fiber bijection
is asserted, and no proper-map or compactness certificate is assumed.
\end{proof}
