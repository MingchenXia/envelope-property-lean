\section{The actual reduced closure of an original point}

For an arbitrary original scheme $X$ and $\eta\in X$, always use the literal
native ideal sheaf
\[
 I_\eta=\operatorname{vanishingIdeal}
   \bigl(\overline{\{\eta\}}\bigr).
\]
In particular the scheme below is exactly $I_\eta.\mathrm{subscheme}$,
with its original native closed inclusion. No integral component, scheme-image
presentation, chosen generic point, or comparison certificate is supplied.

\begin{lemma}[The native vanishing-ideal subscheme is reduced]
\label{lem:scheme-point-closure-vanishing-reduced}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemePointClosureIntegral.isReduced_vanishingIdeal_subscheme}
\leanok
For any original closed subset $Z$ of any original scheme $X$, the actual
subscheme of its native vanishing ideal is reduced.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the native subscheme affine cover, whose chart on an original affine
open $U$ is exactly the spectrum of
$\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)/I_Z(U)$. The native definition of $I_Z(U)$ is the
vanishing ideal of the inverse image of $Z$ in that original affine spectrum.
Mathlib's vanishing-ideal theorem makes this actual ideal radical. Its proved
quotient criterion therefore makes the actual quotient ring reduced.
Each original quotient chart is reduced, and the native open-cover criterion
makes their actual subscheme reduced. No hypothesis on reducedness of $X$
is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[The original point lifted to its actual reduced closure]
\label{def:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemePointClosureIntegral.pointClosureLift}
\leanok
The point $z_\eta$ of the literal $I_\eta.\mathrm{subscheme}$ is the original
subtype point $\eta$ with its membership in $\overline{\{\eta\}}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
The native subscheme has underlying space literally the subtype of the
support of its ideal sheaf. The native vanishing-ideal support is the original
closed subset. An original point belongs to the closure of its own singleton,
so these native facts define the subtype point without choice.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The canonical lift maps to the same original point]
\label{lem:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift-image}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemePointClosureIntegral.pointClosureLift_image}
\leanok
\uses{def:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift}
The original native inclusion sends $z_\eta$ to $\eta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The native inclusion is literally the subtype value map. Its value on the
preceding subtype constructor is definitionally the original $\eta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The same canonical lift is generic]
\label{thm:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift-generic}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemePointClosureIntegral.isGenericPoint_pointClosureLift}
\leanok
\uses{def:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift,lem:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift-image}
The point $z_\eta$ is a generic point of the whole literal
$I_\eta.\mathrm{subscheme}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The original subtype inclusion is inducing. Its native closure-preimage
formula computes the closure of $\{z_\eta\}$ as the inverse image of
$\overline{\{\eta\}}$. Every point of this actual subscheme belongs to that
same original closure, by its original subtype membership and the native
vanishing-ideal support identity. The inverse image is therefore the whole
subscheme, exactly the definition of being its generic point.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The actual reduced point closure is integral]
\label{thm:scheme-point-closure-integral}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemePointClosureIntegral.isIntegral_pointClosure}
\leanok
\uses{lem:scheme-point-closure-vanishing-reduced,thm:scheme-point-closure-canonical-lift-generic}
For every original point of any original scheme, the literal
$I_\eta.\mathrm{subscheme}$ is integral.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding generic point makes the whole subscheme irreducible,
by Mathlib's native generic-point theorem. The first lemma makes
this same actual subscheme reduced. Mathlib's proved criterion
for an integral scheme now applies to that same subscheme.
\end{proof}
