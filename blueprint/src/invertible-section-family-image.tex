\section{The actual image of a whole original section family}

\begin{proposition}[Evaluation of an original family]
\label{prop:actual-invertible-section-family-image}
Let $L$ be an invertible module on an arbitrary scheme $X$, and let
$(s_i)_{i\in A}$ be any original global sections of $L$.
Tensoring the actual free module $\mathcal O_X^{(A)}$ by $L^\vee$
gives a quasicoherent module canonically isomorphic to
$\bigoplus_{i\in A}L^\vee$. Transport the coproduct of the actual
evaluations against $s_i$ across this isomorphism. The resulting map
$\alpha:\mathcal O_X^{(A)}\otimes L^\vee\to\mathcal O_X$ has
whole image ideal
\[
 \operatorname{im}(\alpha)=\sum_{i\in A}\operatorname{im}(s_i).
\]
Neither a finite-family nor a Noetherian hypothesis is needed. In
particular the empty family has zero image, and using all original
global sections gives the entire original base ideal.
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.unitTensorDualIso, EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.freeTensorDualIso, EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.freeTensorDual_isQuasicoherent, EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.familyEvaluation, EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.familyEvaluation_imageIdeal, EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.allSections_imageIdeal}
\uses{prop:actual-quasicoherent-image-factorization}
\leanok
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The actual free sheaf is locally free and hence quasicoherent.
Tensoring by $L^\vee$ preserves quasicoherence. Since this tensor
functor is an equivalence with inverse tensoring by $L$, it preserves
coproducts. Its canonical coproduct comparison, followed by the
original unit isomorphism on each summand, gives the asserted
isomorphism and the indicated evaluation map.

Write $J$ for the sum of the original individual evaluation ideals.
Each individual evaluation factors through $J$ by its actual image
containment. The coproduct universal property combines these whole
factors, and transporting along the same isomorphism gives a whole
factorization of $\alpha$ through $J$. The image-factorization
criterion proves $\operatorname{im}(\alpha)\subseteq J$.
Conversely, precompose the actual source-to-image factor of $\alpha$
with each original coproduct inclusion transported by the inverse
isomorphism. Its composite with the image inclusion is precisely the
original evaluation against $s_i$. Applying the same criterion shows
that every individual image is contained in
$\operatorname{im}(\alpha)$, and taking their sum proves equality.
These coproduct arguments include an empty index type. The final
assertion is the definition of the base ideal using all global
sections.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One actual projective finite evaluation family]
\label{cor:actual-projective-section-family-image}
Let $X$ admit a projective factorization over a Noetherian affine base,
and let $L$ be any original invertible module on $X$. There exists a
finite subset $G\subseteq\Gamma(X,L)$ such that the actual evaluation
map constructed above for the original sections in $G$ has image equal
to the entire original all-global-section base ideal of $L$.
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.InvertibleSectionFamilyImage.exists_projective_finite_imageIdeal}
\uses{prop:actual-invertible-section-family-image, prop:projective-line-base-ideal-finite-global-generators}
\leanok
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The original projective geometry and Noetherian base give one finite
family $G$ whose individual section ideals sum to the whole original
base ideal. Apply the preceding proposition to the actual subtype of
sections belonging to $G$. Its image is the sum over that subtype,
which is exactly the sum of the individual ideals over $s\in G$, and
hence the whole original base ideal. This uses the internally derived
finite family, without any supplied generation or image certificate.
\end{proof}
