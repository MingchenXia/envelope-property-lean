\section{The original rounded ideal of an SNC divisor}

\begin{definition}[Rounded product of actual SNC components]
\label{def:rounded-snc-original-product}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product}
\leanok
For a finite divisor family $F=(F_i)$ on $Y$, multiplicities $a_i\in\mathbb N$,
and $q\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$, put
\[
 R(F,a,q)=\prod_i\mathcal I_{F_i}^{\lfloor qa_i\rfloor}.
\]
This is a product of the original ideal sheaves on the whole original scheme.
\end{definition}
\begin{proof}
Products and nonnegative integral powers of quasicoherent ideal sheaves are
quasicoherent ideal sheaves. The finite product therefore defines an ideal
sheaf on $Y$, including the empty family, which gives $\mathcal O_Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The actual local rounded monomial]
\label{lem:rounded-snc-original-stalk}
\uses{def:rounded-snc-original-product}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product_stalkIdeal,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.exists_product_stalk_coordinates,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product_ideal}
\leanok
Suppose the actual component ideals through $y$ have stalks
$\mathcal I_{F_i,y}=(z_{c(i)})$. Then
\[
 R(F,a,q)_y=\Bigl(\prod_{i:y\in F_i}z_{c(i)}^{\lfloor qa_i\rfloor}\Bigr).
\]
The same formula holds on every affine open on which these components are
cut out by the corresponding sections and all other components are absent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Stalks and affine ideal values commute with finite products and powers.
A component absent from the point has unit stalk; a component absent from
the affine open has unit ideal there. Delete these unit factors. Powers of
principal ideals are generated by powers of their original generators,
and the product of finitely many principal ideals is generated by their
product. This proves both assertions with the stated original sections.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[SNC support and invertibility of the rounded ideal]
\label{lem:rounded-snc-original-invertible}
\uses{def:rounded-snc-original-product}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product_isIdealOfSncDivisor,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product_isInvertible}
\leanok
If $F$ is SNC, then $R(F,a,q)$ is the ideal of an SNC divisor. If $Y$ is
smooth over a field, this actual ideal sheaf is invertible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the same actual family $F$ with multiplicities $\lfloor qa_i\rfloor$
in the definition of an SNC divisor ideal. Each component ideal of an SNC
family on a smooth scheme is invertible: in a regular local ring a
coordinate parameter is a nonzero divisor and generates its stalk ideal.
Finite products and nonnegative powers of invertible ideals are invertible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prime monomial rounding is intrinsic]
\label{lem:rounded-snc-prime-monomial-independent}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.PrimeMonomialRounding.associated_rounded_products}
\leanok
Let $A$ be a domain. Let $(p_i)$ and $(r_j)$ be finite families of prime
elements, pairwise nonassociated within each family. If
$(\prod_i p_i^{a_i})=(\prod_j r_j^{b_j})$, then
$(\prod_i p_i^{\lfloor qa_i\rfloor})=(\prod_j r_j^{\lfloor qb_j\rfloor})$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equality of these nonzero principal ideals is association of their
generators. Expand each generator into its finite multiset of prime
factors, repeating $p_i$ exactly $a_i$ times and $r_j$ exactly $b_j$ times.
Prime factor uniqueness in a cancellative commutative monoid gives equal
multisets after passing to association classes. Pairwise nonassociation
means that the multiplicity of the class of $p_i$ is exactly $a_i$, and
similarly for $r_j$. Thus every occurring class has the same multiplicity
on both sides. Replace each multiplicity $m$ by $\lfloor qm\rfloor$.
The resulting multisets of association classes are equal; multiplying them
shows association of the rounded generators, hence equality of their
principal ideals. No unique factorization hypothesis on arbitrary
elements of $A$ is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Independence of the original SNC presentation]
\label{thm:rounded-snc-original-independent}
\uses{lem:rounded-snc-original-stalk,lem:rounded-snc-prime-monomial-independent}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product_eq_of_presentations}
\leanok
On a smooth scheme over a field, any two actual SNC presentations
$J=\prod_i\mathcal I_{F_i}^{a_i}=\prod_j\mathcal I_{G_j}^{b_j}$ give
$R(F,a,q)=R(G,b,q)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At every point take the actual regular parameters supplied by each SNC
family. The parameters are prime elements of the original regular local
domain, and distinct assigned parameters are nonassociated. The preceding
stalk formula identifies the two presentations of $J_y$ with the actual
principal monomials in these parameters. The prime monomial lemma gives
equality of their rounded principal ideals. Equality at all original
stalks gives equality of the original ideal sheaves.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The rounded original ideal and all adapted charts]
\label{thm:rounded-snc-original-ideal}
\uses{thm:rounded-snc-original-independent,lem:rounded-snc-original-invertible,lem:rounded-snc-original-stalk}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_eq_product,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_isIdealOfSncDivisor,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_isInvertible,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_ideal,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_zero,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_one}
\leanok
Let $J$ be an actual SNC divisor ideal on a smooth scheme $Y$. Its SNC
condition alone determines an actual invertible ideal
$\mathcal O_Y(-\lfloor qF\rfloor)$, where $J=\mathcal O_Y(-F)$.
For every actual SNC presentation and every affine etale chart adapted to
that presentation, its entire affine ideal is generated by the original
coordinate monomial $\prod_i p_i^{\lfloor qa_i\rfloor}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the finite SNC family and multiplicities whose existence is exactly
the SNC divisor ideal condition, and form the rounded product. The
independence theorem removes the choice. The invertibility lemma gives
actual invertibility. For any other presentation replace the chosen
rounded product by that presentation's product, and apply the affine
formula. Thus chart compatibility is a consequence of equality with one
whole global ideal sheaf, with no chart compatibility premise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Smooth pullback of the original rounded ideal]
\label{lem:rounded-snc-original-smooth-pullback}
\uses{thm:rounded-snc-original-independent}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.product_comap,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.rounded_comap}
\leanok
In characteristic zero, let $g:Z\to Y$ be smooth, with $Y$ smooth over a
field. The canonical rounded ideal of $g^*J$ is the actual pullback of the
canonical rounded ideal of $J$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an actual SNC presentation of $J$. Its actual component pullbacks
form an SNC family because $g$ is smooth, and present the original $g^*J$
with the same multiplicities. Pullback of ideal sheaves commutes with
finite products and nonnegative powers, so the rounded product pulls back
to that family's rounded product. Presentation independence identifies it
with the canonical rounded ideal on $Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[An actual finite adapted cover for both original ideals]
\label{thm:rounded-snc-original-finite-charts}
\uses{thm:rounded-snc-original-ideal}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RoundedSncIdealSheaf.exists_finite_rounded_etale_cover}
\leanok
Let $Y$ be quasi-compact and smooth of relative dimension $n$ over a
perfect field, and let $J$ be an actual SNC divisor ideal. There is a
finite actual affine etale coordinate cover on which both $J$ and its
canonical rounded ideal have their original monomial equations, with
multiplicities respectively $a_i$ and $\lfloor qa_i\rfloor$ in the SAME
original coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose the finite SNC presentation supplied by $J$ itself. At every closed
point its original regular parameters spread to actual affine etale
coordinates adapted to all components, shrinking so components absent at
the point are absent on the whole open. The smooth scheme is Jacobson, so
these opens cover every point by specialization to a closed point.
Quasi-compactness gives a finite subcover. The original unrounded formula
is the product formula with integral multiplicities $a_i$; the canonical
rounded formula is the preceding chart theorem for the same family.
\end{proof}
