\section{The original Euler characteristic under actual affine pushforward}

\begin{definition}[The actual field-cohomology pushforward comparison]
\label{def:fc-scheme-coherent-euler-pushforward-comparison}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemeCoherentEulerAffine.fieldCechPushforwardIso}
The whole original field-linear Cech cohomology on the inverse-image
cover is identified with that of the actual original pushforward by
the genuine native-to-ordered comparison, the actual whole ordered-Cech
pushforward isomorphism, and the inverse native-to-ordered comparison.
The literal original composite structure morphism supplies its field action.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Actual affine cohomology and pushforward]
\label{thm:fc-scheme-coherent-euler-affine}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SchemeCoherentEulerAffine.eulerCharacteristic_eq_finrank_sections, EnvelopeProperty.SchemeCoherentEulerAffine.eulerCharacteristic_pushforward}
\uses{def:fc-scheme-coherent-euler-pushforward-comparison, thm:fc-scheme-coherent-euler-bounded}
On an actual affine scheme the original coherent Euler characteristic
is the dimension over the original field of its actual global sections.
For an actual affine morphism $g:D\to C$ between quasi-compact separated
schemes, an original quasicoherent module $M$ has
$\chi(D,M)=\chi(C,g_*M)$, retaining the literal original composite
structure morphism to the original field.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the affine assertion use the actual singleton cover by the whole
scheme. Positive cohomology vanishes by its actual one-element index
bound, and actual degree-zero cohomology is actual global sections.
For pushforward, pull back an actual finite affine cover of $C$ to $D$.
It remains a finite affine cover because the original morphism is affine.
The genuine ordered-Cech pushforward isomorphism identifies the entire
original complexes on these two covers, with the literal composite
coefficient map. Native-to-ordered Cech homotopy equivalences and the
original scalar restriction identify every whole field cohomology group.
The two covers have the same index bound, so their finite Euler sums
agree. Cover independence identifies them with the original intrinsic
Euler characteristics.
\end{proof}
