\subsection{Finite free etale norm products under base change}
\label{subsec:etale-norm-base-change}

The affine finite free etale case of FHX
\texttt{lem:finite-flat-norm} is treated here. The source's general
finite flat local-length multiplicities, global regularizable functions,
and passage to decreasing limits remain outside this construction.

For a morphism $A\to B$ of commutative $R$-algebras and an arbitrary
commutative $R$-algebra $S$, let $A'=S\otimes_R A$ and $B'=S\otimes_R B$.
The actual structural map sends $s\otimes a$ to $s\otimes\varphi(a)$
and defines the $A'$-algebra structure on $B'$. There is a genuine
$A'$-algebra equivalence
\[
 A'\otimes_A B\simeq B',\qquad
 (s\otimes a)\otimes b\longmapsto s\otimes\varphi(a)b.
\]
The inverse sends $s\otimes b$ to $(s\otimes1)\otimes b$.
Finite freeness, module finiteness and etaleness of $A\to B$ each
imply the corresponding property of $A'\to B'$. When $B$ is finite
free over $A$, its entire determinant norm forms and scalar norms
commute with this actual scalar extension.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the tensor map of the scalar identity and $A\to B$ to construct
the displayed structural ring homomorphism and its induced algebra action.
The structural map and the original-element inclusion agree on the
$A$ coefficients; their commuting images therefore give the forward
balanced tensor homomorphism. The scalar and original-element inclusions
give the inverse homomorphism. On nested pure tensors, its composition
with the forward map sends $(s\otimes a)\otimes b$ to
$(s\otimes1)\otimes\varphi(a)b=(s\otimes a)\otimes b$ by the balanced
relation. The other composition sends $s\otimes b$ to itself.
Tensor induction proves both inverse identities on all elements.
The forward map preserves every $A'$ scalar, hence is an $A'$-algebra
equivalence. Actual tensor base change and transport along this equivalence
produce the three algebraic properties. Finally determinant polynomial
base change followed by invariance under this genuine algebra equivalence
proves the whole norm-form identity. The scalar determinant argument is
identical, using the actual scalar norm base-change identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weighted determinant norm products commute with base change]
\label{spectrum:etale-norm-products-base-change}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.weightedFibreProduct_baseChange,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.scalarFibreProduct_baseChange}
\leanok
\uses{spectrum:whole-norm-form-base-change,spectrum:algebra-norm-base-change,
spectrum:weighted-etale-norm-without-infinitude,spectrum:scalar-etale-norm}
Let $A\to B$ be a finite free etale map of commutative rings, let $C$
be any commutative $A$-algebra, and let $x'$ be a real seminorm point
of $C$. Put $x=x'|_A$, and write $b'_j=1\otimes b_j$ in $C\otimes_A B$.
For any finite family $b_j$ and nonnegative radii $r_j$,
\[
 \prod_{y'\text{ over }x'}
 \left(\max_j |b'_j|_{y'}r_j\right)^{m_{y'}}
 =
 \prod_{y\text{ over }x}
 \left(\max_j |b_j|_y r_j\right)^{m_y}.
\]
The same equality holds for the scalar products $\prod|b|^m$.
No bijection between the two fibres is asserted or required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Finite freeness and etaleness persist under the actual tensor base
change. Both products are the weighted coefficient norms of the actual
determinant norm forms. The determinant identity gives the entire
polynomial equality $P_{C\otimes_A B}=P_B\otimes_A C$, and pullback of
the seminorm $x'$ agrees with $x$ on every coefficient. Thus the weighted
coefficient norms coincide. For the scalar statement, the identical
argument uses the genuine scalar determinant norm identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Scalar extension of the original affine morphism]
\label{spectrum:etale-norm-products-scalar-extension}

\leanok

Let $R\to S$ be any map of commutative coefficient rings and let
$A\to B$ be a finite free etale morphism of $R$-algebras. Set
$A'=S\otimes_R A$ and $B'=S\otimes_R B$. The same weighted product
identity holds for the actual affine map $A'\to B'$ at every
$x'\in\mathcal S(A')$, with $x$ its restriction to $A$ and $b'_j=1\otimes_R b_j$.
It therefore holds on prescribed-base relative spaces whenever the
new prescribed seminorm restricts to the original one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The tensor scalar extension map sends $s\otimes a$ to
$s\otimes \varphi(a)$. The genuine cancellation equivalence
$(S\otimes_R A)\otimes_A B\simeq S\otimes_R B$ sends
$(s\otimes a)\otimes b$ to $s\otimes \varphi(a)b$ and its inverse
sends $s\otimes b$ to $(s\otimes1)\otimes b$.
The defining balanced tensor relations show these maps are well-defined;
checking all pure tensors proves both inverse identities. They preserve
the $A'$ action, so the determinant norm forms agree as entire
polynomials over $A'$. The preceding base-change theorem and this
algebra equivalence identify the two weighted coefficient norms.
The actual point norm formula on $B'$ recovers its weighted fibre
product with its own residue-degree multiplicities. There is no
comparison by an assumed bijection of analytic fibres.
Compatible prescribed coefficients make the actual restriction point
lie in the original relative space, without changing the coefficient
or fibre-product equality just proved.
\end{proof}
