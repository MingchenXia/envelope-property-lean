\begin{proposition}[Finite Cech gluing of a natural scalar-extension comparison]
\label{prop:fc-finite-cech-flat-base-change}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.FiniteCechFlatBaseChange.tensorFunctor,EnvelopeProperty.FiniteCechFlatBaseChange.componentMap,EnvelopeProperty.FiniteCechFlatBaseChange.componentMap_restrict,EnvelopeProperty.FiniteCechFlatBaseChange.tensorEquiv,EnvelopeProperty.FiniteCechFlatBaseChange.tensorEquiv_tmul,EnvelopeProperty.FiniteCechFlatBaseChange.isIso_component}
\leanok
\uses{lem:fc-flat-kernel-base-change}
Let $F$ be an actual sheaf of $R$-modules and $G$ an actual sheaf of
$S$-modules on the same original topological space. Let
$\psi:S\otimes_R F\to G$ be a natural transformation of the actual
presheaves, where scalar extension is taken section by section. Fix an
original open $W$. Suppose $S$ is flat over $R$, the original opens
$U_i$ form an actual finite cover, and the actual components of $\psi$
are isomorphisms on every $W\cap U_{i_0}$ and every
$W\cap U_{i_0}\cap U_{i_1}$. Then its component on sections over that
same $W$ is an isomorphism. Taking $W$ to be the whole space gives the
whole global-section comparison. No Cech differential square is a premise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The native whole term equivalences identify degree zero and degree one
with their original products of intersection sections. Finite tensor
product distribution and the actual local isomorphisms give true whole
term equivalences. On each pure tensor, their coordinates are precisely
the original local components of $\psi$. Naturality of $\psi$ commutes
with every original tuple-deletion restriction. Its linearity preserves
the actual alternating sum, proving the entire first-differential square.
The genuine sheaf condition identifies sections on the same $W$ with each actual
first-differential kernel, with the true augmentation. Flat kernel
preservation gives the whole equivalence. Composing with the actual
augmentation and checking every tuple coordinate identifies this
result with the original component $\psi$ on that same $W$.
\end{proof}
