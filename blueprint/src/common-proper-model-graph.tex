\section{The actual common proper model of a prescribed birational graph}

\begin{theorem}[Common proper graph with both original structural diagrams]
\label{thm:common-proper-model-graph}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.graph, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.graph_fst, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.graph_snd, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.model, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.inclusion, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.embedding, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.firstProjection, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.secondProjection, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.embedding_isClosedImmersion, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.inclusion_isOpenImmersion, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.inclusion_isDominant, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.inclusion_embedding, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.inclusion_firstProjection, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.inclusion_secondProjection, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.structure_triangle, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.firstProjection_isProper, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.secondProjection_isProper, EnvelopeProperty.CommonProperModelGraph.model_isIntegral}

Let $f:X\to S$ and $g:Y\to S$ be proper morphisms, with
$X$ integral. Suppose a genuine partial isomorphism
$e:U\xrightarrow{\sim}V$ between dense opens of $X$ and $Y$
is compatible with the two structural maps. Assume the inclusion
$U\hookrightarrow X$ is quasi-compact. Let
\[
 h:U\longrightarrow X\times_S Y,
 \qquad h=(\iota_U,e\iota_V),
 \qquad W=\operatorname{Im}_{\mathrm{sch}}(h).
\]
Then $W$ is integral and its actual two projections
$p:W\to X$ and $q:W\to Y$ are proper and satisfy $pf=qg$.
The canonical map $j:U\to W$ is a dense open immersion and
\[
 jp=\iota_U,\qquad jq=e\iota_V.
\]
Thus the same integral $W$ maps properly to both original
models, with all original structural maps retained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The graph $h$ followed by the first product projection is the
open immersion $\iota_U$. Since the second structural map is
separated, the graph is an immersion. It is quasi-compact
because its composite with the first projection is
quasi-compact. The canonical factorization into its schematic
image is therefore a dense open immersion $j$, and the image
inclusion $i:W\hookrightarrow X\times_SY$ is a closed immersion.
The nonempty open $U$ of the integral scheme $X$ is integral.
The schematic image of a quasi-compact map from an integral
scheme is integral: its structure sheaf is reduced by the
schematically dominant image factor, and its underlying space
is the irreducible closure of the image of $U$.

Define $p=i\operatorname{pr}_X$ and
$q=i\operatorname{pr}_Y$. Each product projection is a base
change of one of the original proper maps, so both composites
are proper. The product equation gives $pf=qg$. The two
displayed identities follow from the actual image factorization
$ji=h$ and the defining graph equations.

\end{proof}

This construction is the ordinary geometric common-domination
step. For test configurations, equivariant actions, projectivity
and normalization must be retained in subsequent comparisons;
none of those extra conclusions is part of the present statement.
