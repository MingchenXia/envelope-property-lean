\section{Canonical actual Cartier generic unit sections and their true model norm}

\begin{definition}[Original generic Cartier inclusion frame]
\label{def:actual-cartier-generic-inclusion-frame}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.genericIdealInclusionIso}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.genericIdealInclusionIso_hom}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.genericIdealUnitSection}
\leanok
\uses{cor:actual-cartier-ideal-nonzero-generic-inclusion}
When the original intrinsic Cartier ideal norm at an original
relative scheme-valued point is nonzero, the actual whole generic
pullback inclusion is an isomorphism. Follow it by the native
original pullback-unit isomorphism. Its inverse applied to the
original unit section gives a canonical actual section $s_I$ of
the same original residue-field pullback Cartier line.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Actual generic unit section and model norm]
\label{lem:actual-cartier-generic-unit-section-model-norm}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.genericIdealUnitSection_coefficient}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelLineNorm_genericIdealUnitSection_mul}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelLineNorm_genericIdealUnitSection}
\leanok
\uses{def:actual-cartier-generic-inclusion-frame,def:scheme-line-actual-model-hom-norm,lem:scheme-line-actual-model-unit-norm,thm:actual-cartier-inclusion-model-norm}
The entire original generic inclusion frame maps $s_I$ to the
original unit section. Its actual model norm satisfies
$\|I\|_y\|s_I\|_y=1$, where $\|I\|_y$ is the original Cartier
inclusion morphism norm, equivalently the original intrinsic ideal
norm. Consequently the actual model norm of $s_I$ is its reciprocal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The unit-section identity is the original inverse-hom identity of
the actual generic inclusion frame, evaluated on the actual unit
section. The original structure-line model norm of its image is
therefore one. The genuine original module-morphism scaling
identity says that the same norm is the actual inclusion norm
times the original model norm of $s_I$. The inclusion norm equals
the intrinsic model ideal norm by the proved Cartier metric
identity. Its nonvanishing follows from the given intrinsic norm
nonvanishing. Cancellation in the original nonnegative scalar
field yields the reciprocal formula. Thus the entire rational
trivialization and its actual norm are derived, with no extra
section or metric certificate supplied.
\end{proof}
