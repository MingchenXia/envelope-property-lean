\section{The genuine original projective family has its actual positive diagonal coordinate algebra}
\label{sec:original-projective-positive-diagonal-algebra}
\begin{lemma}[Degree one in every factor is exactly an original coordinate tuple]
\label{lem:actual-multimodel-all-one-coordinate-tuples}
\uses{def:gl-model}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.OriginalProjectivePositiveDiagonalAlgebra.oneDegree,EnvelopeProperty.OriginalProjectivePositiveDiagonalAlgebra.tupleMon,EnvelopeProperty.OriginalProjectivePositiveDiagonalAlgebra.tupleMon_monDeg,EnvelopeProperty.OriginalProjectivePositiveDiagonalAlgebra.tupleMon_zCoeff,EnvelopeProperty.OriginalProjectivePositiveDiagonalAlgebra.exists_tupleMon_of_oneDegree}
\leanok
For any genuine finite multigraded model, a coordinate monomial of degree
one in every factor selects exactly one original coordinate in each
factor. Conversely every tuple of original coordinates gives such a
monomial. Its actual coefficient is exactly the product of those same
original coordinates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A tuple gives exponent one on its selected coordinate in each factor
and zero on all other coordinates. Summing the exponents in each factor
gives one, and multiplying all coordinate powers gives the product of
the selected coordinates. Conversely, the nonnegative integer exponents
in each factor have sum one. Exactly one exponent is therefore one and
all others are zero. Choose that coordinate in each factor. Equality of
every exponent shows that the original monomial is precisely the one
constructed from this tuple.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[The actual original family diagonal is generated by exactly its original coordinate products]
\label{thm:actual-original-family-positive-diagonal-algebra}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.OriginalProjectivePositiveDiagonalAlgebra.model_positiveDiagonal_eq_adjoin_products}
\leanok
\uses{lem:actual-multimodel-all-one-coordinate-tuples,thm:finite-projective-native-field-models,lem:gl-veronese,thm:finite-segre-native-coordinate-algebra}
Let $X$ be integral and let $q_j:X\to\mathbf P_k^{d_j}$ be a nonempty
finite original projective family with the same original base triangle
and an original closed first factor. Form its actual reconstructed
multigraded model over the entire same original $K(X)$, with its internally
chosen original factor pivots. The strictly positive diagonal of degree
one in every factor has algebra exactly
\[
 k\left[U\prod_jq_j^*(X_{j,a_j}/X_{j,i_j})\mid (a_j)_j\right]\subset K(X)[U].
\]
This is equality of the whole actual diagonal algebra. No diagonal,
coordinate-generation or model-comparison premise is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The actual reconstructed family algebra is, by its original construction,
generated by precisely its original homogeneous factor coordinates.
Every homogeneous component of every element of this algebra is a linear
combination of coordinate monomials with that same degree. In the degree
$n$ positive diagonal, split each factor's $n$ selected coordinates into
$n$ groups containing one coordinate from each factor. Each monomial is
then a product of $n$ diagonal coordinate products. Thus every homogeneous
diagonal term lies in the algebra generated by those degree-one products.
Summing all homogeneous terms gives the entire diagonal algebra. Conversely,
every such degree-one product is an original model monomial of the required
diagonal degree, so it lies in that diagonal algebra. Finally the preceding
lemma identifies all degree-one diagonal monomials with all tuples of the
same original factor coordinates. These coordinates are literally the
original pulled ratios in the same original function field, by construction
of the genuine family model. This gives the displayed equality in both
directions, with the original coefficient action unchanged.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[The actual positive diagonal and actual original Segre algebra coincide]

\begin{proof}
The preceding theorem identifies the entire actual family diagonal with

coefficient actions are the action of the actual Segre base composite,

Thus the two algebras coincide, with no change of field, coordinates or
coefficient action.
\end{proof}
