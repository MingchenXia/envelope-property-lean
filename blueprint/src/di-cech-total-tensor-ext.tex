\section{Actual tensor total maps are detected on original bidegrees}

\begin{theorem}[Native pure-tensor bidegree detection]
\label{thm:di-cech-total-tensor-ext}
\uses{def:di-cech-total-cup}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.DI.CechTotalTensorExt.totalTensor_hom_ext}
Let $T$ be the same original first-quadrant Cech total complex and
let $D$ be any actual module sheaf on its original scheme. Two actual
morphisms $T^a\otimes T^b\to D$ agree if their composites with the
tensor maps of every pair of original bidegree inclusions agree.
The bidegrees range over $q+j=a$ and $r+j'=b$. This is a native
morphism detection statement, requiring no cohomology, exactness of
sections, covering condition, or differential hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In a fixed total degree, the original identity is the finite sum of
its native bidegree projections followed by native inclusions.
Use these two original identity decompositions in the two inputs
of the actual tensor functor. Additivity of the original tensor
morphism map in each input expands the tensor identity as a finite
sum. Functoriality identifies each summand with the tensor of the
two projections followed by the tensor of the two inclusions.
Composing this actual identity decomposition with either morphism
expresses that morphism as the corresponding finite sum of its
bidegree composites. The assumed equalities identify every pair of
summands, so the two entire sheaf morphisms agree.
\end{proof}
