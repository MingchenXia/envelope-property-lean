\subsection{Actual Laurent Gauss seminorms and whole annulus bounds}
\begin{theorem}[The actual Laurent Gauss seminorm at a positive radius]
\label{spectrum:actual-laurent-gauss-valuation}
\uses{spectrum:relative-principal-open,spectrum:gauss-valuation}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_toLaurent,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_C,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_T_nat,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_T_neg_nat,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_T,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.coeff_mul_T,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.coeff_toLaurent_nat,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.coeff_toLaurent_of_neg,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.le_laurentGauss_val,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_eq_sup,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurentGauss_val_eq_zero_iff,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurent_val_T}
\leanok
Let $A$ be any commutative ring, $v:A\to\R_{\ge0}$ an actual
multiplicative ultrametric seminorm, and $r>0$. The genuine
localization $A[T,T^{-1}]=A[T][1/T]$ has a unique seminorm
restricting to the radius-$r$ polynomial Gauss seminorm. It preserves
all original coefficients, has $|T^j|=r^j$ for every $j\in\mathbb{Z}$,
and for every actual Laurent polynomial $f=\sum_j a_jT^j$ satisfies
\[
 |f|_{v,r}=\max_{j\in\operatorname{supp}f}v(a_j)r^j.
\]
The maximum of an empty coefficient support is zero. If $v$ is
faithful, this Laurent seminorm is faithful. Neither a domain nor
a faithful coefficient seminorm is needed for its construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The genuine polynomial Gauss seminorm takes $T$ to the actual
nonzero number $r$. Extend it through the real principal
localization constructor; the whole localization inverse proves
uniqueness on every Laurent element. Multiplicativity and the
identity $T^jT^{-j}=1$ give all integer-power values and preserve
all original polynomial values.

For any $f$ the actual Laurent polynomial shift theorem constructs
$n\ge0$ and $p\in A[T]$ with $p=fT^n$. Thus
$|f|_{v,r}=|p|_{v,r}/r^n$. The whole original Laurent coefficient
shift identity identifies the coefficient of $T^i$ in $p$ with
$a_{i-n}$; all coefficients of $f$ below $-n$ vanish. Apply the
proved full polynomial Gauss coefficient maximum and divide by
the positive $r^n$. This gives the displayed full finite maximum,
including coefficients annihilated by $v$. If $v$ is faithful,
zero Laurent value forces every coefficient to vanish, hence
$f=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Whole Laurent bounds on genuine annulus points]
\label{spectrum:actual-laurent-annulus-endpoint-bound}
\uses{spectrum:actual-laurent-gauss-valuation}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.Spectrum.laurent_val_le_max_endpoint}
\leanok
For arbitrary commutative $A$, prescribed $v$, positive
$\rho,r$, and an ACTUAL Laurent seminorm $x$ with EVERY
coefficient equal to $v$, if
$\rho\le |T|_x\le r$, then for EVERY original Laurent
polynomial $f$,
\[
 |f|_x\le \max(|f|_{v,\rho},|f|_{v,r}).
\]
No domain, faithfulness or chosen polynomial-value identity is
required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The true unit $T$ has nonzero value at EVERY Laurent point.
Multiplicativity gives the entire integer-power formula
$|T^j|_x=|T|_x^j$, including negative exponents via the
actual inverse unit. Expand the WHOLE original $f$ as its
finite sum $\sum_j a_jT^j$ and apply the genuine ultrametric
finite-sum bound. For $j\ge0$, its monomial value is at most
$v(a_j)r^j$; for $j<0$, it is at most
$v(a_j)\rho^j$, because inversion reverses the actual
positive coordinate bound. The first theorem bounds each of
these real coefficient terms by the corresponding ENTIRE
endpoint Laurent value of $f$. Taking the maximum bounds
all monomials and thus the whole original finite sum.
The zero polynomial and empty support obey the same argument.
\end{proof}
