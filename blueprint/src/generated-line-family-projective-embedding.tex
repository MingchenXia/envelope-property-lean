\begin{theorem}[Actual original generated lines on one genuine projective product embedding]
\label{thm:generated-line-family-projective-embedding}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.GeneratedLineFamilyProjectiveEmbedding.polynomialFamily, EnvelopeProperty.GeneratedLineFamilyProjectiveEmbedding.polynomialFamily_isProjectiveFactorization, EnvelopeProperty.GeneratedLineFamilyProjectiveEmbedding.exists_projective_product_embedding}

Let the same original integral $X$ be natively projective over its
original coefficient ring. For any finite family of actual
original invertible lines with actual finite global generating
families, one can internally construct an actual closed immersion
of the same original $X$ into a finite product of actual original
polynomial projective spaces over the same coefficient ring.
For every given original line, its whole pullback of the original
$\mathcal O(1)$ from the corresponding factor is isomorphic to that
same original line. For every original generator, its selected
coordinate section pulls back through this whole isomorphism to
that same original generator. The product structure triangle is the original
structure morphism exactly; no combined embedding, whole line
comparison, or coordinate-matching certificate is supplied.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use genuine native projectivity of the original $X$ to obtain one
actual closed polynomial projective embedding with its exact
original coefficient triangle. For each given original finite
globally generated line, the actual original generated-line
projective-map theorem constructs its entire original map to an
original polynomial projective space, the whole pullback
$\mathcal O(1)$ isomorphism, and every original coordinate-section
identity. Put the first original closed embedding and these
actual original maps into one finite family over the same base,
using one extra factor for that original closed embedding.

Take the actual iterated native relative product and its actual
whole combined map. Every factor has its literal polynomial
projective factorization, derived using the identity embedding.
The finite-product theorem proves the same whole combined map
closed because its first unchanged constituent was closed. Its
whole coefficient triangle is the actual triangle of the
constructed morphism over the same original base. Its whole
projection identity on each remaining factor identifies that
entire composite with the corresponding original generated-line
projective morphism. Substitute this true original morphism
equality into the whole line isomorphism and every original
coordinate-section identity already constructed. This retains
every original generator and original line. The actual finite
product geometry is thereby proved; reconstruction of the
original multiprojective homogeneous coordinate ring and its
function field remains a separate comparison problem.
\end{proof}
