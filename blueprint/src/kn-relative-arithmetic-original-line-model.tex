\section{Descent of the same original line on the relative arithmetic model}

\begin{lemma}[The original line and relative embedding descend together]
\label{lem:kn-relative-arithmetic-original-line-model}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.RelativeArithmeticOriginalLineModel.exists_original_line_model}
\uses{lem:kn-relative-arithmetic-original-geometric-model,lem:kn-relative-arithmetic-original-line-expression,lem:kn-relative-arithmetic-scalar-transition,thm:kn-arithmetic-original-line-descent}
\leanok
Under the original relative projective hypotheses, every original
invertible module $L$ has an actual invertible arithmetic model
$M$ on a finite coefficient enlargement of the produced smooth
relative projective model.  Its whole original pullback is exactly
$L$.  The relative very ample model $T$ of the same original
$j^*\mathcal O(1)$, the total smooth dimension, separatedness,
and the actual affine-base tensor fibre are all retained.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the original line as
$L\simeq h^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P_k^b}(1)
\otimes(j^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb P_R^d}(N))^\vee$
using the mixed projective expression.  Start with the produced
relative arithmetic model and its very ample line $T$ recovering
$j^*\mathcal O(1)$.  The original field morphism $h$ descends to a
morphism $h_S:Y_S\to\mathbb P_S^b$ at a finite coefficient
enlargement, because the source model is quasi-compact and
quasi-separated and projective space is finitely presented.
The recovery equation identifies its original pullback with $h$.
Let $T_S$ be the actual pullback of $T$, and define
$M=h_S^*\mathcal O(1)\otimes(T_S^{\otimes N})^\vee$.
Both factors are invertible, so $M$ is invertible.

Pullback commutes with tensor products, duals of invertible
modules, and all tensor powers.  The original identification of
$T$ therefore identifies the original pullback of
$T_S^{\otimes N}$ with
$(j^*\mathcal O(1))^{\otimes N}=j^*\mathcal O(N)$.
The original recovery equation for $h_S$, followed by the whole
projective twist coefficient comparison, identifies its first
factor with $h^*\mathcal O(1)$.  Thus the whole pullback of $M$
is exactly the displayed expression for the original $L$.
The coefficient-transition lemma retains the relative very ample
line and the actual original-field square.  Smoothness of the
same dimension, separatedness, and quasi-compactness are stable
under this coefficient base change.  The modeled affine algebra
is its actual tensor extension, whose original fibre remains $R$
by tensor associativity.
\end{proof}
