\section{The actual Leray comparison from local acyclicity}
\label{sec:kn-nadel-leray-cech}

\begin{lemma}[Positive cohomology through actual inverse-image intersections]
\label{lem:kn-nadel-leray-acyclic-intersections}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelLerayCech.subsingleton_H_iff_ordered_zero,EnvelopeProperty.Vanishing.NadelLerayCech.subsingleton_H_iff_pushforward}
\leanok
Let $f:Y\to X$ be any scheme morphism, let $X$ be separated, and let
$U_i$ be a finite affine open cover of $X$.  Let $M$ be a module on $Y$
whose actual pushforward is quasicoherent.  Suppose that the restriction
of $M$ to every nonempty finite tuple intersection of the inverse images
$f^{-1}U_i$ has zero positive cohomology.  Then, for each $q>0$,
\[
 H^q(Y,M)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H^q(X,f_*M)=0.
\]
The intersections and both modules are the actual original ones; no
affineness of $f$ is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The inverse-image opens cover $Y$.  Their assumed acyclicity on every
tuple intersection makes their actual Cech complex compute positive
cohomology vanishing of $M$.  Its ordered normalized complex has the
same homology, since the finite index set admits a linear order.
The corresponding ordered complex for $f_*M$ is canonically identical:
in every degree the actual sections on $f^{-1}(U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap
U_{i_a})$ are the sections of $f_*M$ on that intersection, with the
same restriction maps and original scalar action.  Inverse image
preserves these finite intersections, giving the entire cochain
isomorphism, including its differentials.

Every tuple intersection of the $U_i$ is affine because $X$ is separated.
Quasicoherence of $f_*M$ gives acyclicity on those intersections.
The same Cech comparison therefore computes its positive cohomology
vanishing.  Transport zero homology through the actual cochain
isomorphism to obtain both implications.

For a principalization, the local acyclicity premise must be proved by
the local vanishing argument.  This comparison does not prove local
vanishing and does not add it to the temporary DI input or to a final
Nadel theorem's assumptions.
\end{proof}
