\begin{theorem}[Whole bounded seminorms extend uniquely to the actual completion]
\label{spectrum:actual-bounded-completion-extension}

Let $A$ be a seminormed commutative ring. Give its actual separated
norm completion $\widehat A$ the genuine completed ring structure.
Every multiplicative ultrametric seminorm $x$ on $A$ satisfying
$|a|_x\leq\|a\|$ for every original element has a UNIQUE
multiplicative ultrametric extension to $\widehat A$ satisfying
$|b|\leq\|b\|$ for every completion element. The extension preserves
all original values, not merely a chosen dense generating family.
The usual boundedness condition $|a|_x\leq C\|a\|$ for some
$C>0$ is equivalent to this contractivity. Neither completeness
nor an ultrametric norm on $A$ is needed for this completion
statement. Empty bounded spectra are allowed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First, usual boundedness implies contractivity by the genuine
positive-power argument: multiplicativity gives
$|a|^m\leq C\|a^m\|\leq C\|a\|^m$; taking positive
$m$th roots and letting $m$ tend to infinity gives
$|a|\leq\|a\|$. Conversely contractivity gives the bound with
$C=1$. This is the existing power-multiplicative bounded-map theorem,
applied to the true map into the completed residue field.

Use the actual completed residue field $\mathcal H(x)$, with the
canonical original ring map $A\to\mathcal H(x)$. Its value on every
$a$ has norm exactly $|a|_x$. The displayed whole bound therefore
makes this actual ring homomorphism contractive: apply it to
$a-a'$ to bound the distance of the images. Hence it is uniformly
continuous and extends through the genuine norm completion to a
continuous ring homomorphism $\widehat A\to\mathcal H(x)$. Actual
completion density proves that its norm remains bounded by the
completion norm, on every completion element. Pull back the genuine
norm valuation of $\mathcal H(x)$ along this entire ring map.
This constructs the claimed ultrametric multiplicative seminorm
and preserves every original value by the actual extension law.
The extension is also the unique continuous RING homomorphism
into this actual $\mathcal H(x)$ extending the given original
ring map: equality of continuous maps on the true dense original
image proves their equality on the entire completion.

For uniqueness, any contractive seminorm on $\widehat A$ is
continuous as a real-valued function. Indeed its ultrametric
triangle inequality implies the usual reverse triangle estimate
$\bigl||b|-|b'|\bigr|\leq|b-b'|\leq\|b-b'\|$.
Two such actual seminorms agreeing on every original element agree
on the true dense original image, hence everywhere. This argument
also handles a nonseparated original seminorm: zero-norm original
elements have zero value, so the separated completion loses no
value of a bounded point.
\end{proof}

\label{spectrum:actual-bounded-completion-homeomorphism}

The genuine restriction along $A\to\widehat A$ is a homeomorphism
from the contractive bounded spectrum of $\widehat A$ to that of
$A$, for their actual evaluation topologies. Both bounded spectra
are compact Hausdorff. If $A$ has an actual coefficient-ring action,
this restricts to a homeomorphism for every prescribed coefficient
seminorm, with all original coefficient values preserved. No
coefficient bound or extension-existence certificate is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each actual contractivity condition $|a|\leq\|a\|$ is closed in
the genuine evaluation topology. The spectrum is a closed subspace
of the full product of nonnegative-real evaluation coordinates;
restricting every coordinate to its true finite norm interval
gives compactness by the actual coordinate-bound compactness
theorem. Hausdorffness is inherited from the spectrum.

The actual coefficient completion map is a ring homomorphism, so
its pullback on spectra is continuous; its norm equality on every
original element preserves contractivity. The preceding true
extension and uniqueness theorem proves bijectivity on whole
points. A continuous bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff
space is a genuine homeomorphism. For prescribed coefficients,
restriction and extension preserve every original scalar value;
restrict this actual homeomorphism to their exact coefficient
loci, with their genuine subspace topologies. No separate inverse
or unrelated topology is postulated.
\end{proof}
