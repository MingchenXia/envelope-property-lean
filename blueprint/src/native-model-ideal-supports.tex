\subsection{Native supports and true center support of intrinsic model ideals}
\label{subsec:native-model-ideal-supports}

The native scheme support and the model center have different roles.
Vanishing of a model ideal norm detects the original native support;
strictly smaller than one concerns closed-ideal support at the true model
center. Finite generation is load-bearing for the converse of the latter
statement and is supplied explicitly on an actual affine center neighborhood.
No continuity or anti-continuity of the center map is inferred.

\begin{lemma}[Actual scheme closed-ideal support in germs and residue values]
\label{scheme:actual-ideal-support-in-germs}
\lean{AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.stalkIdeal_le_maximalIdeal_iff_mem_support,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.SchemeValuedPoint.sectionIdealNorm_eq_zero_iff_mem_support}
\leanok
\uses{scheme:ideal-sheaf-data-stalk,scheme:valued-points}
For actual ideal-sheaf data $I$, its intrinsic stalk ideal at $x$ is
contained in the genuine local maximal ideal exactly when $x$ belongs to
its actual scheme closed support. For any native valued point supported
in an actual affine $U$, the norm of $I(U)$ in the original section
seminorm is zero exactly when the original native point belongs to that
same actual scheme support.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an actual affine neighborhood of $x$ and use the established stalk
ideal equality with its genuine germ-image. The ideal-map inequality is
equivalent to all ideal section germs belonging to the local maximal
ideal. A germ is nonunit exactly when the original point lies outside
the section's basic open. The actual scheme zero-locus criterion and
Mathlib's actual ideal-sheaf support restriction therefore prove the first
equivalence. For a native valued point, ideal norm zero means every ideal
section has value zero. Its valuation on the actual residue field is zero
only on zero, so original section evaluation vanishes exactly outside its
basic open. The same genuine zero-locus and support criteria prove the
second equivalence without any bound on the original section seminorm.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genuine native zero locus of intrinsic model ideals]
\label{scheme:native-model-ideal-zero-locus}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelIdealNorm_eq_zero_iff,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelIdealLog_eq_bot_iff,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.isClosed_modelIdealNorm_zeroLocus}
\leanok
\uses{scheme:actual-ideal-support-in-germs,scheme:native-model-ideal-function,
scheme:valued-point-topology}
At a native point of a proper scheme over the explicitly bounded
prescribed base, the intrinsic model ideal norm is zero, equivalently its
logarithm is $-\infty$, precisely when its \emph{original native support}
belongs to the actual scheme support of $I$. This native zero locus is
closed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a true affine neighborhood of the model center. The original
native support belongs to it by the proved specialization, and the intrinsic
norm equals the actual affine section ideal norm. Apply the preceding
native residue-value equivalence. The actual extended logarithm has
bottom value precisely at zero, proving the logarithmic statement. The
scheme closed support of $I$ is genuinely closed and the original native
support projection is continuous. Its inverse image is therefore closed,
exactly the just-proved zero locus. This does not require global continuity
of the full model ideal function or replace the center in its definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[True center closed-ideal criterion with explicit finite generators]
\label{scheme:model-ideal-strict-center-criterion}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.center_mem_support_of_modelIdealNorm_lt_one,
EnvelopeProperty.Berkovich.RelativeSchemeValuedPoint.modelIdealNorm_lt_one_iff_of_generators}
\leanok
\uses{scheme:actual-ideal-support-in-germs,scheme:native-model-ideal-generators,
scheme:proper-center-local-bounds}
An intrinsic norm smaller than one always implies that the true model
center belongs to the scheme closed support of $I$. If a supplied finite
family generates $I(U)$ on an actual affine center neighborhood, the
converse holds too. These statements concern the genuine model center,
rather than vanishing at the original native support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every intrinsic stalk ideal element has valuation at most its actual ideal
norm. When that norm is smaller than one, all such values are smaller
than one. The true center-stalk valuation detects its actual maximal
ideal by this strict inequality, so the stalk ideal lies in that maximal
ideal. The first lemma proves membership of the center in the scheme support.

With actual finite generators, the established maximum formula says that
the norm is smaller than one precisely when all generator values are.
The original generator values equal the actual center-stalk germ values.
Strict-value detection turns this into maximal-ideal membership for all
generators; the genuine germ-image span theorem makes it equivalent to
containment of the entire stalk ideal. Apply the first lemma again. The
finite-family maximum includes the empty family, hence the genuine zero
ideal. Without finite generation, individual values below one need not
bound their supremum strictly below one; no such converse is asserted.
\end{proof}
