\section{Dimension of a proper closed source}

\begin{theorem}[Dimension drop for the original closed map]
\label{thm:seshadri-original-proper-closed-map-dimension-drop}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.SeshadriAmplenessCriterionClosedMapDimension.closedImmersion_dimension_drop}
\leanok
\uses{lem:seshadri-proper-closed-dimension-drop}
Let $j:Y\to X$ be an actual closed immersion of original schemes, with
$X$ irreducible and $j$ not surjective. For a nonnegative integer $d$,
if the original topological Krull dimension of $X$ is at most $d+1$,
then the original topological Krull dimension of $Y$ is at most $d$.
In particular this applies to the integral schemes in the lower-dimensional
Nakai induction. Integrality of $Y$, projectivity, a base field, and an
incoming comparison of dimensions or point closures are not needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The native closed-immersion theorem makes the underlying original map
$j$ a closed embedding. Form the actual closed subset
$Z=\operatorname{range}(j)$ of $X$, with its original subtype topology.
If $Z=X$, every original point of $X$ would have a preimage under $j$,
contrary to the assumed nonsurjectivity. Thus $Z$ is a proper closed subset.

The native embedding of this same $j$ constructs its actual homeomorphism
$Y\simeq Z$. The existing homeomorphism invariance of topological Krull
dimension gives $\dim Y=\dim Z$. Apply the already proved proper-closed-set
dimension-decrease theorem to this literal $Z\subsetneq X$ and the
original bound $\dim X\leq d+1$. It yields $\dim Z\leq d$.
Combining these two actual conclusions gives $\dim Y\leq d$.
The strict-chain dimension argument and the topological homeomorphism
criterion are reused without reproof. No new image scheme, chosen
point-closure comparison, or target-dimension certificate is assumed.
\end{proof}
