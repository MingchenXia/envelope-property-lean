\subsection{Surjectivity of the actual Rees projection}

\begin{theorem}[The original nonzero-domain Rees projection is surjective]
\label{scheme:actual-rees-projection-surjective}
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.ReesPresentation.chartImmersion,
EnvelopeProperty.ReesPresentation.chartImmersion_comp_projection,
EnvelopeProperty.ReesPresentation.genericLift,
EnvelopeProperty.ReesPresentation.genericLift_comp_projection,
EnvelopeProperty.ReesPresentation.projection_surjective}
\leanok
\uses{scheme:actual-rees-presentation-proper,algebra:actual-rees-chart-fraction-embedding}
Let $A$ be any domain and $(g_i)_{i\in I}$ a finite original family with
$g_j\ne0$ for some actual index $j$. The SAME original Rees quotient scheme
projection is proper and surjective over $\operatorname{Spec}A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the actual homogeneous degree-zero localization at the ORIGINAL class
of $Y_j$. Its genuine ring map into $\operatorname{Frac}A$ sends every
homogeneous original numerator of degree $d$ to its original evaluated
numerator divided by $g_j^d$. Its original coefficient restriction is the
canonical injective map $A\to\operatorname{Frac}A$. Apply Spec to the whole
ring map and compose with the actual Proj chart immersion. The true chart
pullback square for the homogeneous quotient, followed by the polynomial
Proj structure-map chart formula, identifies the WHOLE composite with
$\operatorname{Spec}(A\to\operatorname{Frac}A)$, since every original
coefficient is preserved. This gives an actual source point above the
original zero prime of $A$, rather than a valuation or support substitute.

The original projection is proper, hence its image is closed. The closure
of the original zero prime in $\operatorname{Spec}A$ is all of that space:
the closure is its original ideal zero locus, and the ideal is zero.
The image therefore contains the whole coefficient scheme. This proves
surjectivity of the SAME actual projection. It needs no Noetherianity or
algebraically closed field, and adds neither a comparison certificate nor
a proper-surjective witness as a hypothesis.
\end{proof}
