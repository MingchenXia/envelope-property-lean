\section{Laurent reconstruction retains the original coordinate indices}
\label{sec:c0n-section-polynomial-rename}

\begin{lemma}[Reconstruction through the actual universe-lifted coordinates]
\label{lem:c0n-polynomial-original-index-reconstruction}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RelativeHomogeneousSectionLift.originalPolynomialToLaurent_injective,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RelativeHomogeneousSectionLift.exists_original_polynomial_of_empty_laurent}
Let $R$ be the original commutative ring, $\sigma$ the original finite
linearly ordered coordinate index set, and $e\in\mathbb N$. An actual
degree-$e$ Laurent element on the empty coordinate intersection, with
coordinates indexed by the genuine universe lift $\operatorname{ULift}
\sigma$, is the Laurent image of
$\operatorname{rename}(\operatorname{up})(F)$ for an original homogeneous
polynomial $F\in R[x_i\mid i\in\sigma]_e$. The entire original ring $R$,
every coordinate, and every coefficient are retained. The map from these
original polynomials to the lifted Laurent algebra is injective.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the already-proved arbitrary-commutative-ring empty-intersection
Laurent reconstruction to obtain an actual homogeneous polynomial $G$
indexed by $\operatorname{ULift}\sigma$ with the prescribed Laurent image.
Define $F=\operatorname{rename}(\operatorname{down})(G)$. Actual polynomial
renaming preserves homogeneous degree because every variable is still sent
to a variable of weight one. Moreover
$\operatorname{rename}(\operatorname{up})(F)=G$: the actual native
rename-composition theorem reduces this to
$\operatorname{up}\circ\operatorname{down}=\operatorname{id}$ on the
entire lifted index set. Substitution gives exactly the prescribed Laurent
image, with no representation or matching premise.

The polynomial-to-Laurent map is genuinely injective over an arbitrary
commutative ring. Renaming by $\operatorname{up}$ is injective since
$\operatorname{up}$ itself is injective. Their composite is therefore
injective, including the zero ring and a single original coordinate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The actual original localized Laurent maps are faithful]
\label{lem:c0n-polynomial-localization-laurent-faithful}
\leanok
\lean{EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RelativeHomogeneousSectionLift.originalLocalizationToLaurent_injective,EnvelopeProperty.Char0.RelativeHomogeneousSectionLift.originalAwayToLaurent_injective}
Let $f\in R[x_i\mid i\in\sigma]$ have unit image in the SAME lifted
Laurent algebra. The actual original localization map from $R[x]_f$ to
that Laurent algebra is injective. Its restriction to the actual native
homogeneous degree-zero localization is also injective.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the native localization injectivity criterion: it suffices to detect
equality on original polynomial numerators. Equal original localized
numerators have equal Laurent images by applying the actual localization
map. Conversely, equality of those Laurent images is equality of the
original polynomials by the preceding genuine injectivity theorem, hence
equality of their original localized images. This verifies BOTH directions
of the native criterion without a domain or reducedness assumption.
The actual homogeneous localization embeds injectively in the actual
ordinary localization by its proved native fraction-value map. Compose
that injection with the preceding genuine localization injection.
\uses{lem:c0n-polynomial-original-index-reconstruction}
\end{proof}
