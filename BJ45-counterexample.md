# A counterexample to BJ22 Theorem 4.5 in its original generality

**Status: mathematical proof note, not formalized in Lean.** The construction uses only the definition of psh functions by decreasing Fubini–Study approximation. Its membership proof does not use BJ22 Theorem 4.5, an envelope theorem, or a decreasing-limit theorem for already-psh functions.

Here BJ22 refers to Boucksom–Jonsson, [*Global pluripotential theory over a trivially valued field*](https://www.numdam.org/item/AFST_2022_6_31_3_647_0.pdf), Annales de la Faculté des Sciences de Toulouse 31 (2022), 647–836. We use Definition 4.1 and compare with Theorem 4.5, specifically property (PSH2) and its consequence (PSH2a).

We work over $k=\mathbb C$ with its trivial absolute value. All logarithmic section norms use the trivial metrics. Thus they are nonpositive. For a coherent ideal $\mathfrak a$, write $\log|\mathfrak a|$ for its homogeneous ideal function.

## Statement

There exist an integral projective non-unibranch surface $X$, a rational numerical class $\theta$, an ample line bundle $A$, and a generically finite upper semicontinuous function $u:X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$ such that

$$
 u\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta+\varepsilon[A])
 \quad\text{for every rational }\varepsilon>0,
 \qquad
 u\notin\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta).
$$

Consequently the constant sequence $u_n=u$, considered in the classes $\theta_n=\theta+n^{-1}[A]$, contradicts property PSH2 of BJ22 Theorem 4.5. This is a failure of numerical-class closedness. It does not contradict fixed-class decreasing stability or the unibranch case.

## 1. Point pinching and line bundles

Let $Y$ be a smooth projective variety and let $Z\subset Y$ be a finite reduced set, partitioned into disjoint pinch groups. The Ferrand pushout identifies each group to one point and gives a scheme $X$ with a finite birational normalization map $\nu:Y\to X$, an isomorphism away from $Z$. Locally its functions are the functions upstairs whose values agree within each group.

Every line bundle on $Y$ descends after choosing identifications of its fibers within each group. A section descends precisely when its values match under those identifications. In particular, every section vanishing on all of $Z$ descends. This follows from the patching description of locally free modules over the defining fiber-product rings. One can also see it directly: trivialize the line bundle near the finite set, adjust the frames to the chosen fiber identifications, and use matching tuples as the local module downstairs.

The pushout is proper and of finite type. Descend any ample line bundle from $Y$; it remains ample because ampleness descends under finite surjective maps. Hence $X$ is projective. Pullback identifies the numerical class spaces: it is surjective because line bundles descend, and injective because intersection degrees on curves are detected after finite pullback.

These facts are supported by [Ferrand, *Conducteur, descente et pincement*, §2.2 and Theorem 5.4](https://doi.org/10.24033/bsmf.2455), [Stacks, pushouts of schemes](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0ECH), and [Stacks, finite-surjective descent of ampleness, Lemma 30.17.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B5V).

### Elementary FS descent lemma

Fix an ample line bundle $A$ on $X$, sufficiently large that

$$
 H^0(Y,\nu^*A)\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{z\in Z}(\nu^*A)_z
$$

is surjective and $\mathcal I_Z\otimes\nu^*A$ is generated by global sections away from $Z$. These conditions hold for a sufficiently large power of any ample bundle.

Suppose

$$
 p=\frac1m\max_j\log|s_j|,
 \qquad s_j\in H^0(Y,\nu^*(mL)),
$$

is a homogeneous FS function, for a rational line bundle $L$ on $X$, and its values agree within every pinch group. Then, for every integer $N\ge1$, the **same function** $p$ is the pullback of a downstairs homogeneous FS function in

$$
 L+\frac{1}{mN}A.
$$

**Proof.** A homogeneous FS function has value either $0$ or $-\infty$ at a closed point. Let $V$ be the span of its defining sections. In a group where the value is $-\infty$, all sections vanish at all points. In a group where the value is $0$, evaluation on $V$ is nonzero at each point. Since the field is infinite, choose a basis of $V$ avoiding the finitely many evaluation hyperplanes of the latter groups. Every basis section then vanishes on all points or on none of the points in each group. Changing basis preserves the maximum norm: both change-of-basis matrices have coefficients of absolute value at most one.

For a basis section $s$, let $T_{s,N}\subset H^0(Y,\nu^*A)$ be the linear subsystem imposing that the values of $s^N t$ match within each group on which $s$ does not vanish. All coefficients in these matching conditions are nonzero. Surjective evaluation therefore provides an allowed nonzero value at every point of $Z$. Moreover $T_{s,N}$ contains $H^0(Y,\mathcal I_Z\otimes\nu^*A)$, so it has no base points away from $Z$. It is basepoint-free on all of $Y$, and for any basis of this subsystem,

$$
 \max_{t\in T_{s,N}}\log|t|=0.
$$

Each $s^N t$ descends to a section of $mNL+A$. Hence

$$
 \frac1{mN}\max_{s,t}\log|s^N t|
 =\frac1m\max_s\log|s|=p.
$$

This proves the exact representation. No closure theorem is involved. $\square$

## 2. The surface and the function

Let

$$
 Y=\operatorname{Bl}_{p_1,p_2}\mathbb P^2,
$$

where $p_1\ne p_2$, with exceptional curves $E_1,E_2$. Let $H$ be the pullback of a line and set $M=H-E_2$. The pencil $M$ is basepoint-free.

Choose distinct points

$$
 y_1\in E_1,\qquad y_2,y_3\in E_2,
 \qquad y_4\notin E_1\cup E_2,
$$

and a section $s_M$ whose divisor passes simply through $y_4$, avoids $E_1$, and meets $E_2$ away from $y_2,y_3$. Such choices are obtained from a line through $p_2$, avoiding $p_1$, followed by choosing $y_2,y_3$ away from its tangent direction and $y_4$ on its strict transform.

Pinch $y_1\sim y_2$ and $y_3\sim y_4$. This gives an integral projective surface $X$ with normalization $Y$. Choose a descended line bundle $L_\theta$ with

$$
 \nu^*L_\theta\simeq\mathcal O_Y(H+E_1),
 \qquad \theta=c_1(L_\theta).
$$

Put $d=1/2$, write $\ell_i=\log|s_{E_i}|$ and $\ell_M=\log|s_M|$, and define

$$
 \chi(s)=1-\sqrt{1-s}\quad(s\le0),
 \qquad
 \Phi=\ell_1+d\ell_2+d\chi(\ell_M).
$$

Set $\chi(-\infty)=-\infty$. The function $\Phi$ is continuous with values in $\mathbb R\cup\{-\infty\}$. It equals $-\infty$ at all four pinched points and is therefore fiber constant. It descends to an extended-real continuous function $u$ on $X^{\mathrm{an}}$, since the finite proper surjection $\nu^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a quotient map. It has value $0$ at the generic trivial valuation, so it is generically finite.

For later use, if $v_4$ is the exceptional divisorial valuation of the blowup at $y_4$, normalized by $v_4(\mathfrak m_{y_4})=1$, then

$$
 \Phi(t v_4)=d\bigl(1-\sqrt{1+t}\bigr),
 \qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\Phi(t v_4)}t=0.
 \tag{1}
$$

## 3. Nonmembership in the limiting class

Suppose, to the contrary, that $u\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta)$ according to BJ22 Definition 4.1. Take a defining decreasing net

$$
 f_j=\frac1{m_j}\max_k\{\log|s_{jk}|+\lambda_{jk}\}
 \downarrow u,
 \qquad c_1(L_j)\to\theta,
 \qquad s_{jk}\in H^0(X,m_jL_j).
$$

We evaluate their pullbacks on $Y$. Since

$$
 \nu^*\theta\cdot E_1=-1,
$$

for all sufficiently late $j$ every defining section vanishes along $E_1$: an effective divisor not containing $E_1$ has nonnegative intersection with $E_1$. Thus every section vanishes at $y_1$, and the first pinching forces it to vanish at $y_2$.

Fix one such index $j_0$ and put $a=1/m_{j_0}>0$. Let $v_2$ be the exceptional valuation of the blowup at $y_2$. Taking spatial recession in $f_j\le f_{j_0}$ gives, for every $j\ge j_0$,

$$
 -\frac1{m_j}\min_k\operatorname{ord}_{y_2}(s_{jk})
 =\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{f_j(t v_2)}t
 \le -a.
$$

Hence every defining section has ambient vanishing order at least $m_j a$ at $y_2$.

But $\nu^*\theta\cdot E_2=0$. Choose a later $j$ with $\nu^*c_1(L_j)\cdot E_2<a$. If a defining section restricted nontrivially to $E_2\simeq\mathbb P^1$, its vanishing order at $y_2$ would be at least its ambient order $m_j a$, whereas its total degree would be

$$
 m_j\nu^*c_1(L_j)\cdot E_2<m_j a.
$$

This is impossible. Every defining section therefore vanishes on all of $E_2$, hence at $y_3$, and the second pinching forces it to vanish at $y_4$. For this single FS upper bound there is a finite constant $C_j$ with

$$
 u(t v_4)\le f_j(t v_4)\le C_j-\frac{t}{m_j}.
$$

Dividing by $t$ and using (1) yields $0\le-1/m_j$, a contradiction. Thus

$$
 u\notin\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta).
 \tag{2}
$$

The argument applies to arbitrary nets, arbitrary real weights, and arbitrary rational line bundles whose numerical classes tend to $\theta$.

## 4. Explicit FS approximations in every nearby class

Enlarge the ample line bundle $A$ from the descent lemma so that $\mathfrak m_{y_2}\otimes\nu^*A$ is also globally generated. Fix a rational $\varepsilon>0$, and write

$$
 B=L_\theta+\varepsilon A.
$$

We will construct a decreasing sequence of **downstairs FS functions in this one fixed rational line bundle** $B$, converging to $u$.

### Rational tangent functions

For rational $0<a\le d/2$, define

$$
 \lambda(a)=d-a-\frac{d^2}{4a}\le0,
 \qquad
 Q_a=\ell_1+d\ell_2+a\ell_M.
$$

The elementary tangent formula is

$$
 g(r):=d(1-\sqrt{1+r})
 =\sup_{a\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,d/2]}
 \{-ar+\lambda(a)\},\qquad r\ge0.
 \tag{3}
$$

Each $Q_a$ is exactly a downstairs homogeneous FS function in $L_\theta$. Indeed its natural section class is $E_1+dE_2+aM$, and the difference is the semiample class

$$
 H+E_1-(E_1+dE_2+aM)
 =(1-d)H+(d-a)M.
$$

After clearing denominators, multiply the canonical section product by a generating family of this difference. The resulting sections all vanish at all four points: $E_1$ supplies $y_1$, $E_2$ supplies $y_2,y_3$, and $s_M$ supplies $y_4$. They therefore descend to $mL_\theta$, and the generating family contributes the zero FS function. Tensoring with a generating system of a multiple of $\varepsilon A$ presents the same $Q_a$ in $B$.

### An exact FS carrier in the nearby class

Let

$$
 h=\log|\mathfrak m_{y_2}|,
 \qquad
 \Psi=\ell_1+\delta h,
$$

where $\delta$ is rational and $0<\delta<\min\{d,\varepsilon\}$. By global generation, $h$ is homogeneous FS upstairs in $\nu^*A$. The function $\Psi$ is homogeneous FS in the class $E_1+\delta\nu^*A$, with values $-\infty$ at $y_1,y_2$ and $0$ at $y_3,y_4$. It is fiber constant.

Apply the exact descent lemma to $\Psi$. It becomes a downstairs FS function with an extra rational twist $\tau A$, where $\tau>0$ can be arbitrarily small. Choose $\tau<\varepsilon-\delta$. The difference from $B$ has pullback

$$
 H+(\varepsilon-\delta-\tau)\nu^*A,
$$

which is ample. Thus the difference is ample downstairs, by finite pullback, and a generating family of a multiple contributes zero. Consequently $\Psi$ itself is exactly a downstairs FS function in $B$.

Because $\mathfrak m_{y_2}\supseteq\mathcal O_Y(-E_2)$, one has $h\ge\ell_2$. Since $\delta\le d$ and these functions are nonpositive,

$$
 d\ell_2\le\delta h,
 \qquad
 \Phi\le\ell_1+d\ell_2\le\Psi.
 \tag{4}
$$

### A decreasing sequence

Put $U_n=\max\{\Phi,\Psi-n\}$, so $U_n\downarrow\Phi$. These are only auxiliary pointwise functions; no psh assertion is used for them.

Set

$$
 R_n=(1+n/d)^2-1,
 \qquad h_n=2^{-n-2},
 \qquad c_n=2^{-n}.
$$

By (3) and compactness of $[0,R_n]$, choose a finite rational tangent family $S_n\subset(0,d/2]$ such that

$$
 T_n(r):=\max_{a\in S_n}\{-ar+\lambda(a)\},
 \qquad
 g(r)-h_n\le T_n(r)\le g(r)
 \quad(0\le r\le R_n).
$$

Every tangent is globally below $g$. Define the exact downstairs FS functions in $B$

$$
 F_n=\max\Bigl\{\max_{a\in S_n}(Q_a+\lambda(a)),\ \Psi-n\Bigr\},
 \qquad G_n=F_n+c_n.
$$

We claim

$$
 U_n-h_n\le F_n\le U_n.
 \tag{5}
$$

The upper bound follows from the tangent inequality. Where $\Phi\le\Psi-n$, the carrier term gives equality $F_n=U_n$. At a point where $\Phi>\Psi-n$, all terms relevant to subtraction are finite; putting $r=-\ell_M$, (4) gives

$$
 \Phi-\Psi=d\ell_2-\delta h+g(r)\le g(r).
$$

Hence $g(r)>-n$, which implies $r<R_n$. The chosen uniform tangent approximation gives the lower bound in (5). At the pole loci of $\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_M$, either the carrier tail gives equality or both sides are $-\infty$; no subtraction of two infinite quantities is required.

Since $h_n+c_{n+1}\le c_n$, (5) yields

$$
 G_{n+1}
 \le U_{n+1}+c_{n+1}
 \le U_n+c_{n+1}
 \le F_n+h_n+c_{n+1}
 \le G_n.
$$

Moreover $U_n\downarrow\Phi$ and $c_n,h_n\to0$, so $G_n\downarrow\Phi$, including all pole values. These are genuine downstairs FS functions in the fixed class $\theta+\varepsilon[A]$; at the generic trivial valuation their limit is zero. BJ22 Definition 4.1 directly proves

$$
 u\in\operatorname{PSH}(X,\theta+\varepsilon[A]).
 \tag{6}
$$

Together, (2) and (6) prove the counterexample.

## Dependency audit

The proof uses projective point pinching and line-bundle patching; finite-surjective descent of ampleness; global generation after a sufficiently large ample twist; elementary linear algebra over an infinite trivially valued field; elementary convex tangent approximation; and the definition of psh functions by decreasing FS limits. The nonmembership argument uses only intersection theory on a smooth surface, orders of vanishing, and monotonicity of a defining FS net.

No application of any decreasing-limit theorem for already-psh functions occurs. The outer counterexample is the constant sequence $u_n=u$ in the classes $\theta+n^{-1}[A]$.
